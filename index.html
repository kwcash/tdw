How the oldest method of winning was rediscovered, and what it costs a free society to have forgotten it.

China, then

A broken army with no industry, no navy, and no money ran every domain it could reach and took the country in thirteen years.

America, then

China, now

America, now

The argument

Two movements separated by geography, era, and enemy won from nothing by fighting in every domain at once, on a horizon long enough for the combination to compound, against enemies who were formidable inside single domains and could not combine them.

That method is not Chinese. Its clearest modern demonstration was run by the United States. It is being run now, against the West, and it is working. And the reason it works is not something the adversary built. It is something the West built for itself, over roughly a century, one defensible decision at a time.

A note on terms

The Chinese Communist Party is not China. The Chinese people are this method's first and longest-suffering victims, not its authors, and no argument in this book requires or permits the reader to confuse the two. The subject here is a method, who understands it, and what it costs a free society not to.
