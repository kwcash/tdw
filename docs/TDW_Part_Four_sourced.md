# PART FOUR: The Stratagems

*The oldest playbook, running at machine speed*

## Introduction: The Oldest Playbook

The West watches global friction and counts separate emergencies. A hacked server. A frozen supply chain. A stolen patent. A diplomatic insult. Each gets its own headline, its own agency, its own hearing.

The Chinese Communist Party watches the same board and reads a codebook. The crises are not separate. They are instructions, executing as written.

For two thousand years, Chinese statecraft has carried a codified set of thirty-six stratagems.[^1] Western analysts file them under philosophy or folklore. That is a category error. The stratagems are an engineered taxonomy of leverage. Thirty-six tested methods for reaching a strategic objective through deception, indirect force, and the exploitation of an opponent's own structure.

Through most of history, human friction capped their reach. Running one deception across the economic, the informational, and the diplomatic at the same time took a bureaucracy that never tired and a network that never leaked. People tire. Networks leak. The stratagems worked, but the human mind set their speed limit.

Artificial intelligence erased the limit. Feed the thirty-six into a machine that processes the planet's data in real time, and they stop being history. They become the operating software of Total Domain War, running across all twelve domains at once.

This chapter walks all thirty-six, so that no maneuver in the cases ahead arrives unnamed. Six of them, one from each group, get a full profile, because they carry the most weight in the campaign you are about to read. The rest get a line apiece. Each entry carries its classical principle, its documented modern use, and a Western wartime parallel — because the point of this chapter is not that the stratagems are Chinese. It is that they are the grammar of strategy, and the West has spoken it fluently when it had to.

## The Architecture of Leverage: Six Groups

The thirty-six are not a flat list. They divide into six groups of six, and the division is not decorative. Each group fits a different position on the board. Read which group the adversary reaches for, and you have read how the adversary rates its own strength.

**Groups I and II belong to the actor holding the advantage.** The work is Superiority and Confrontation, and the goal is to win cheaply, by misdirection rather than force.

**Groups III and IV belong to the contest itself.** The work is Attack and Confusion. Degrade the opponent's capability, foul its decision loops before any decisive clash, and pull away the foundations its power stands on.

**Groups V and VI belong to the actor inside a stronger host or under hard pressure.** The work is Control and Desperation. Capture the host's institutions from within, or retreat in good order and rebuild for the next round.

So the group is a tell. A Group I move says the Party believes it already holds the high ground. A Group VI move, including the open instruction to retreat, says the Party is buying time. Name the group, and you have named the phase of the campaign you are standing in.

What follows takes the groups in order. Each opens with its anchor stratagem, profiled in full, then lists the other five.

## Group I: Stratagems of Superiority

*Deploy from strength. Win without unnecessary cost by weaponizing the advantage through misdirection rather than direct force.*

### The anchor

**Stratagem 4. Wait at Ease for the Weary Enemy.** This is the Time dimension made operational. The classical text holds the high ground, keeps fresh water, and lets the enemy climb. The Party holds the centennial horizon. It plans in decades and builds in pipelines. The United States plans in two-year electoral cycles and annual budgets. The Party does not need to beat the American system in the field. It waits while that system spends itself on culture wars, debt-ceiling standoffs, and policy that reverses every four years. The West's own short-termism is the power source. Lengthen the Western horizon and the advantage drains away.

Admiral Nimitz ran the Western version of the same patience across the Pacific from 1943 onward. Rather than assault every fortified Japanese garrison in his path, American strategy bypassed the strongest ones, Rabaul and Truk among them, cutting their supply and leaving tens of thousands of defenders to wait on islands the war had already moved past.[^2] No siege was fought where American planners judged the cost not worth paying. The garrisons held their ground until the war ended around them, exhausted by a fight that never came.

### The rest of the group

**1. Deceive the Heavens to Cross the Sea.** Hide the decisive act inside routine, until repetition makes the observer stop looking. Incremental militarization staged as ordinary drills, until the new fact is simply there. The Manhattan Project ran the same principle on its own workforce. Thousands of Los Alamos employees built the most decisive weapon of the war believing, by design, that they were building something else.

**2. Besiege Wei to Rescue Zhao.** Do not strike the enemy's strength. Threaten what it cannot afford to lose. Economic pressure applied at the West's supply vulnerabilities rather than its defended ground. The Doolittle Raid on Tokyo in April 1942 did negligible material damage and struck the one target Japan could not afford to leave undefended, pulling carrier strength home in time to be caught short at Midway two months later.[^3]

**3. Kill with a Borrowed Knife.** Let a proxy deliver the strike and absorb the blame while you keep clean hands. Chinese chemical firms supply fentanyl precursors at industrial scale. The cartels distribute, and tens of thousands of American deaths a year are the strategic output, one step removed from any return address. Washington armed and supplied Chinese Nationalist forces for years before Pearl Harbor precisely so a proxy army, not American troops, would absorb the cost of tying down Japan's forces on the mainland.

**5. Loot a Burning House.** Advance while the adversary is consumed by its own crisis. Protective-equipment leverage and Belt and Road acceleration during the COVID shock. Washington did the same to its closest ally. The Destroyers-for-Bases deal of September 1940 traded fifty aging warships for ninety-nine-year leases on British bases across the Atlantic, negotiated at the exact moment Britain could least afford to refuse.[^4]

**6. Make a Sound in the East, Strike in the West.** Manufacture a loud, credible threat in one place to strip the guard from another. Taiwan drills that absorb attention while the decisive move runs elsewhere. Before Operation Torch in November 1942, American diplomats maintained ordinary relations with Vichy officials across North Africa while the invasion fleet was already at sea, the routine diplomacy itself the eastern sound covering the western strike.

## Group II: Stratagems of Confrontation

*Outmaneuver a stronger adversary through misdirection, manufactured reality, and the exploitation of the adversary's own defensive logic.*

### The anchor

**Stratagem 8. Openly Repair the Gallery Roads, March Secretly Through Chencang.** Han Xin let the enemy watch his soldiers rebuild the mountain roads while his real force slipped through a pass hundreds of miles off. Two tracks run at once. One built to be seen, one built to win. Military-Civil Fusion is the modern version. The visible track is a commercial firm selling commercial products. The hidden track is the legal duty that hands the firm's technology and data to the state on demand. The West debates the visible company while the capability moves down the channel it never watched. You will meet this stratagem again in the Military-Civil Fusion case.

Montgomery built the Western version at El Alamein in October 1942 with a fake pipeline. Royal Engineers laid miles of dummy pipe crawling south, timed to look complete just when a southern attack would need it, while real armor massed in the north disguised as ordinary supply trucks under painted canvas.[^5] German reconnaissance watched the pipeline, read a southern offensive into its progress, and held reserves against an attack that was never coming. The real force broke through in the north, on ground the enemy had judged safe enough to leave lightly held.

### The rest of the group

**7. Create Something from Nothing.** Repeat a fiction at sufficient volume and fidelity until it reads as fact, and the adversary allocates real resources against a false map. Coordinated inauthentic networks and synthetic media manufacture the belief, then let it run. The American 23rd Headquarters Special Troops, the Ghost Army, built entire fake divisions from inflatable tanks and recorded convoy noise, and German reconnaissance filed reports on formations that existed nowhere but on the airwaves.[^6]

**9. Watch the Fire from Across the River.** When rivals are destroying each other, do not intervene. Let the fire consume their strength, then advance into the ruin. Belt and Road expansion during American commitments elsewhere. Congress passed the Two-Ocean Navy Act in July 1940 while Britain and Germany bled each other, building the fleet that would decide the Pacific before American forces had fired a shot in either ocean.[^7]

**10. Hide a Knife Behind a Smile.** Project warmth and mutual benefit until the structural trap is set. The smile is the operation, not the cover. BGI's NIFTY prenatal test, developed in collaboration with the People's Liberation Army and marketed in more than fifty countries, has collected genetic material from millions of women worldwide under a medical banner, and the samples feed a state-run gene bank whose own privacy policy reserves the right to share data for national security purposes.[^8] American vice-consuls in North Africa, Robert Murphy's so-called Twelve Apostles, spent months cultivating ordinary, friendly relations with Vichy French officials, the goodwill itself the preparation for landings those same officials never saw coming.

**11. Sacrifice the Plum Tree to Preserve the Peach.** Surrender a lesser asset to shield the critical one, and make the loss look inevitable. State-firm delistings that block audit access while the larger structure holds. The cruisers lost screening the transports off Guadalcanal in the war's first weeks were the plum tree. The Marine beachhead they died protecting was the peach, and the trade was accepted before it was made.

**12. Take the Opportunity to Pilfer a Goat.** Pocket every incidental gain. No single one is worth a fight, but the aggregate decides. Farmland bought parcel by parcel near military installations, the flock becoming a herd over years. The Alsos Mission followed advancing Allied armies into Germany with no combat mission at all, scooping up scientists, documents, and equipment behind every division that took new ground, a goat pilfered at every stop no single unit was fighting to claim.[^9]

## Group III: Stratagems of Attack

*Use deception, sabotage, and indirect force to degrade the adversary's capability before any decisive engagement.*

### The anchor

**Stratagem 18. Capture the Ringleader to Disband the Bandits.** You do not fight the marching mass. You find the node that gives the network its coherence, and you take that. Hong Kong in 2020 ran this to the letter. Rather than arrest a million marchers and trigger an unmanageable crisis, the Party took the hubs. It jailed Jimmy Lai, froze *Apple Daily*'s payroll, and detained the forty-seven who organized. The marchers remained. The capacity that turned their numbers into political action was gone. A mesh network failed for want of a few critical nodes.

American codebreakers ran the identical logic against a single man in April 1943. Having decoded Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto's flight itinerary, planners did not attempt to degrade the Combined Fleet he commanded. They sent P-38s to intercept his aircraft over Bougainville and killed him directly.[^10] Japan's naval strength in ships and aircraft was untouched that day. The officer whose planning had coordinated it at Pearl Harbor and Midway was gone, and no replacement commanded the same coherence he had built.

### The rest of the group

**13. Stomp the Grass to Scare the Snake.** Pressure a visible node so the hidden network moves and reveals its shape. The probe is intelligence, not the attack. Journalist expulsions used to map who connects to whom. The Dieppe raid of August 1942 failed as an assault and succeeded as a probe, the German response mapping coastal defenses in detail the planners of Normandy two years later could not have obtained any other way.

**14. Borrow a Corpse to Resurrect the Soul.** Inhabit a hollow or discredited institution and run new objectives on its residual legitimacy. Capture of multilateral bodies whose names still command deference. British intelligence gave the stratagem its most literal form in 1943. A homeless man's body, dressed as a fictional Royal Marines officer and released off the Spanish coast carrying false invasion plans, sent Germany reinforcing Greece and Sardinia while the real target, Sicily, went ashore three weeks later against a lighter defense.[^11]

**15. Lure the Tiger Down the Mountain.** Draw the adversary off the ground where its advantages apply onto terrain you control. Gray-zone operations that exploit American institutional friction. Rommel's Afrika Korps fought at the end of a supply line stretched across the Mediterranean, drawn deep into terrain where distance, not German skill, decided the outcome once the pincer from Torch closed behind him.

**16. Let the Enemy Off in Order to Catch Them.** Ease the pressure at the right moment to induce overconfidence and overextension, then tighten. Market access offered as relief from cost pressure, breeding the supply-chain dependency that becomes the trap. Eisenhower let the German counteroffensive of December 1944 drive deep into the Ardennes rather than contest the border outright, then closed on the exposed flanks of the bulge it had created, catching in the open the force that overextension had committed.

**17. Cast a Brick to Attract Jade.** Offer something of modest value to extract something far greater. The Thousand Talents Program pays the salary, the grant, the prestige. The proprietary research and dual-use technology walk out the door through channels the home institution never sees. Britain's Tizard Mission handed America its most advanced radar and jet-engine research in 1940 in exchange for the industrial capacity to build on it at scale, a brick of raw manufacturing power returned as jade in finished weapons within years.[^12]

## Group IV: Stratagems of Confusion

*Destabilize the adversary's operating environment through manufactured chaos and the removal of the foundations its power depends on.*

### The anchor

**Stratagem 24. Borrow the Road to Conquer Guo.** The classical actor asks passage through a neighbor's land under a neutral purpose, then uses the road for a second purpose the neighbor never granted. Huawei and ZTE base stations are the road. The stated purpose is commercial telecommunications. The embedded capability, owed to the state under fusion law, is remote access, traffic intercept, and remote shutdown. The host laid the road for commerce. The guest drove intelligence down it.

Britain and the Soviet Union invoked the same logic against Iran in August 1941, under the stated purpose of securing a supply corridor for war materiel bound for the Eastern Front. Operation Countenance opened the road. What followed was an occupation, Anglo-Soviet forces controlling the country's government and territory for the war's duration and after, the corridor never closed once it was granted.[^13] Iran had leased passage. It received an occupying presence it had never agreed to host.

### The rest of the group

**19. Remove the Firewood from Under the Pot.** Do not attack the fire. Cut the fuel that sustains it, and the flame dies on its own. Gallium and germanium export throttling that starves a downstream industry the West cannot quickly re-source. The 1944 oil campaign detailed in the Criticality dimension is this stratagem run against an entire war machine. Not the tanks, but what moved them.

**20. Muddy the Waters to Catch the Fish.** Flood the field with enough noise that the adversary cannot tell threat from clutter, ally from infiltrator, real from synthetic. Epistemic attacks on elections and institutions, where the goal is not to win the argument but to make agreement impossible. RAF bombers over Hamburg in July 1943 dropped bundles of foil strips, code-named Window, that returned so many false echoes German radar operators could no longer separate an actual bomber stream from static, and the raid went in nearly unopposed.[^14]

**21. Slough Off the Cicada's Golden Shell.** Leave a convincing decoy in place while the actor relocates and reconstitutes undetected. Corporate restructuring that resets the regulator's analysis to zero. A thin rear guard held the Dunkirk perimeter through early June 1940, maintaining fire and radio traffic that looked like an army still defending, while the men it was screening were already gone across the Channel.

**22. Shut the Door to Catch the Thief.** Encircle completely before engaging, so the adversary cannot be reinforced, resupplied, or rescued. The Indo-Pacific port-acquisition network closing the doors the West needs open. American, British, Canadian, and Polish forces closed the Falaise Gap in August 1944, sealing the last exit from Normandy before engaging what was trapped inside, rather than fighting a German army still free to maneuver.

**23. Befriend a Distant State While Attacking a Neighbor.** Buy alignment with distant powers to strip the target of the coalition it would otherwise call on. Global South Belt and Road alignment that shrinks the diplomatic majority the West relies on for enforcement. Washington secured airfield rights at Natal, Brazil, years before either country was formally at war with Germany, buying a distant partner's alignment to deny the Axis any foothold in the hemisphere and to open the shortest air route to Africa.

## Group V: Stratagems of Control

*From growing strength, infiltrate, replace, and redirect the adversary's power structures until they serve the actor's objectives.*

### The anchor

**Stratagem 30. Make the Host and the Guest Exchange Places.** This is the Agency dimension, and it is the move the West least expects, because it is slow. The guest arrives deferential and dependent. Over years it gathers relationships and institutional knowledge until it becomes indispensable, and then it sets the agenda. The Party's two decades inside the technical standards bodies — the ITU, ISO, and 3GPP — is the macro version. Chinese state-linked delegates entered as observers from a developing nation. Today they draft the standards, and they have written surveillance-compatible architecture into the telecommunications rules the host must now obey.[^15] The guest writes the house rules. Watch for this one. It produces no single event a headline can catch, which is exactly why it works.

The United States arrived in this same war in 1942 as the newer partner to an established British global position, deferential to a military tradition and a Mediterranean strategy it had not designed. By 1944 the roles had reversed without any single event marking the change. American production, American manpower, and Eisenhower's command of Overlord itself settled the strategic direction of the war in Europe over British planners' own preferences for where and when the decisive blow should fall. The guest had become the one setting the campaign's terms.

### The rest of the group

**25. Replace the Beams with Rotten Timbers.** Swap the load-bearing supports of the adversary's power for compromised ones. The building stands until weight is applied, then fails. United Front placement in Western academic, policy, and advisory positions, so the captured expertise speaks in the host's own voice. Allied Military Government replaced Fascist-era administrators across liberated Sicily and Italy in 1943 with vetted alternatives before the old structure could be trusted to bear any weight at all.

**26. Point at the Mulberry While Cursing the Locust.** Punish a secondary target so the real audience reads the warning without being addressed. The detention of Michael Kovrig and Michael Spavor as a signal aimed past Canada. The unconditional-surrender declaration at Casablanca in January 1943 was addressed to Germany on its face. Its real audience, by Roosevelt and Churchill's own account, sat in Moscow, and the message was that no separate peace would leave Stalin fighting alone.

**27. Feign Madness Without Losing Balance.** Project weakness or irrationality so the adversary underestimates you and lowers its guard, while the real capability and the real objective stay intact. The "peaceful rise" posture that bought thirty years of cover during the buildout. Britain understated its own fighter production through 1940, letting German intelligence believe the RAF was closer to exhaustion than the output figures showed, an underestimate the Luftwaffe kept paying for over the Channel that summer.

**28. Lure the Enemy onto the Roof, Then Remove the Ladder.** Induce the adversary to commit to a position of dependency, then cut the way back. Manufacturing dependency first, regulatory capture second. Japan's own naval and industrial planning deepened its dependence on American oil for a decade before Washington ever moved to cut it off in 1941, the ladder removed only once the roof was fully occupied.

**29. Deck the Tree with False Blossoms.** Project more strength, scale, or coalition than the actor actually holds, to deter what the real capability could not. State media dressed as independent global outlets. Fortitude North invented an entire fictional British Fourth Army poised against Norway in 1944, radio traffic and all, a coalition of divisions that existed only on paper and held German garrisons in place a continent away from the beaches actually under threat.

## Group VI: Stratagems of Desperate Situations

*From disadvantage, use layered deception and the adversary's own assumptions to survive and reconstitute for the next engagement.*

### The anchor

**Stratagem 36. If All Else Fails, Retreat.** Alone among the thirty-six, this one names withdrawal as doctrine, not failure. A disciplined retreat preserves the force for the next cycle. The lesson for a Western reader is a hard one. When the Party pulls back from a market, abandons a deal, or softens a posture, do not score it as a win. Read it as repositioning. The actor who can retreat in order has not lost. It has chosen the ground for the next engagement.

American forces learned the doctrine the hard way at Kasserine Pass in February 1943, driven back by Rommel in the United States Army's first major defeat of the war.[^16] Command did not score the retreat as the end of the campaign. It reorganized, Patton replaced the officer who had lost the ground, and the same American force returned weeks later to defeat the same German army on the same ground it had just surrendered. The defeat was real. So was the doctrine that treated it as one battle in a longer campaign rather than the campaign's verdict.

### The rest of the group

**31. The Beauty Trap.** Compromise a decision-maker through whatever desire makes them susceptible, whether financial, sexual, ideological, or social. Romantic cultivation of cleared personnel. The Double-Cross System's handlers built each turned German agent's file around whatever made him useful — ideology, money, vanity, self-preservation — and ran him against his own service on exactly that leverage rather than any single formula.

**32. The Empty Fortress.** When genuinely weak, project such confidence that the adversary assumes a trap and declines to press. Confident posture and tight data control during the COVID shock. Britain in the weeks after Dunkirk had lost most of its army's heavy equipment on the beaches and armed its Home Guard with whatever could be found, while projecting, in Churchill's own broadcasts, a defiance so total that an invasion force poised across the Channel never came.

**33. Sow Discord Among the Enemy.** Find the existing fractures in the adversary's coalition and widen them. Turn allies into liabilities and trust into suspicion. Transatlantic alliance fragmentation operations. Allied intelligence worked the widening strain between Rome and Berlin through 1943, encouraging an Italian disillusionment with the alliance that helped bring down Mussolini and take Italy out of the war within months.

**34. Inflict Injury on Yourself to Win Trust.** Accept visible damage to establish credibility and gain access you could not otherwise obtain. Controlled defector and asset operations. The Double-Cross System let its turned agents pass genuine, verifiable, low-value intelligence back to Berlin precisely so the credibility earned by real losses would be spent on the one deception, Fortitude, that mattered.[^17]

**35. Chain the Stratagems.** The meta-stratagem, and the most important of the thirty-six. It gets its own section next.

## The Meta-Layer: Stratagem 35, Chain the Stratagems

A single stratagem is dangerous. Chained, they are decisive. Stratagem 35 is the instruction to operate on the whole system at once, linking maneuvers so the opponent's answer to the first becomes the trap that makes the second unavoidable.

The Belt and Road Initiative is the clearest demonstration. Four stratagems run in sequence.

**Stratagem 17**, cast a brick to attract jade. Offer infrastructure financing below market terms. The economic door opens.

**Stratagem 16**, let the enemy off to catch them later. The terms look generous, so the recipient overextends into deep dependency.

**Stratagem 28**, lure onto the roof, then remove the ladder. When debt service fails, demand the physical infrastructure as collateral.

**Stratagem 30**, make the host and the guest exchange places. The port, the grid, or the network passes to Chinese control. A sovereign recipient is now a logistics and intelligence asset.

Each link is deniable on its own. The West processes a loan here and a default there. The chain produces an encirclement no single link could.

The Allies chained the same way to open the war's decisive front. Operation Overlord was not one deception but a sequence. **Stratagem 1** hid the invasion's true preparation inside routine, ordinary movement orders and false purposes shielding the real buildup. **Stratagem 29** decked the northern flank with a fictional Fourth Army threatening Norway, holding German divisions a continent from the true beaches. **Stratagem 6** had already sounded the diplomatic east in North Africa eighteen months earlier while the western strike went in at Torch, and SHAEF now ran the same principle at far greater scale. **Stratagem 22** closed the door at Falaise, but only after the landing the earlier links had protected succeeded. No single deception won Normandy. The chain did, each link's success the condition the next one needed, exactly as this stratagem describes.

AI changes the physics of the chain. A machine can simulate thousands of these sequences across the global economy in milliseconds and pick the one path of least resistance through an uncoordinated defense. The human planner ran one chain at a time. The machine runs them all and keeps the best.

## How to Read the Rest of This Book

You now hold the lens, and you have seen all thirty-six. From here, every case is the same reflex. When a chapter shows you a frozen supply line, do not file it as a market failure. Name it. Stratagem 19, remove the firewood from under the pot. When you see a flood of synthetic media around an election, do not file it as a moderation problem. Name it. The discipline is simple, and it is the whole point of this chapter. Stop treating each pressure as a fresh surprise. Ask three questions of anything you see. Which stratagem is this? Which group does it come from, and so how does the adversary rate its position? What is it chained to?
