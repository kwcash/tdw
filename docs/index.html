A book by James Rogers

Total Domain War

The oldest method of winning a conflict, rediscovered. How the Chinese Communist Party runs it now, and what it costs a free society to have forgotten it.

China, 1937–1949

Had the method

Land reform, propaganda, the enemy's own corruption, and time. Ran every domain it could reach against an opponent with every material advantage. Won.

China, today

Still has it

The Party never put the instrument down. Twelve domains, one committee, one aim, and a stated horizon reaching to 2049.

America, 1941–1945

Had the method

Industry, science, finance, information, culture, and arms fused into one instrument. Out-produced the Axis powers combined in munitions.

America, today

Cannot run it

Not for lack of money, talent, or allies. Seventeen intelligence agencies produce assessments no one reconciles. One department funds what another forbids.

Three of these record a capability that was present. The fourth records its absence, and it is the one the reader lives in.

The distinction this book will not let go of

China is a civilization several thousand years old. The Chinese people are not the enemy, and no argument in this book requires or permits a reader to think otherwise.

The Chinese Communist Party is a political organization that has ruled the mainland since 1949. It is not China, any more than the Khmer Rouge was Cambodia.

The distinction is not a courtesy. It is the analytical key, because every method described in this book was turned on the Chinese people first. The famine that followed the Great Leap Forward. The Cultural Revolution. Tiananmen. Xinjiang. They are this method's longest-suffering victims, not its authors.

A reader who finishes the book with a grievance against Chinese people has read it exactly backward.

What war means now

Modern conflict does not begin with an invasion. It targets systems rather than territory, and it operates below the threshold that would trigger a response.

Law, capital, supply chains, standards bodies, patents, platforms, and attention are all instruments. Each move is legal, deniable, or too small to answer on its own. The aggregate is a campaign.

The defender loses not because it is weak but because it has assigned each domain to a different office and given no office authority to look across them. The attack does not need to beat any single defender. It runs through the seams.

The twelve domains

  1. KineticPhysical force, most of it employed without being used. The fleet that never sails is still doing work.
  2. EconomicDependency built patiently through trade and supply, then held as a valve.
  3. FinancialCapital routed through structures the investor cannot see through, into firms the same government has flagged.
  4. InformationNot persuasion. The degradation of a society's shared sense of what is real.
  5. PsychologicalThe will to resist, targeted directly, including through relatives who can still be reached.
  6. LegalLaw written in advance to license what is intended, and the slow law of open societies used against them.
  7. AssociationAlliances fractured and abstention purchased, so the target stands alone before anything begins.
  8. CyberNot theft. Quiet access placed inside the systems a rival would need in a crisis, waiting.
  9. CulturalMarket access that makes an editor censor himself before anyone has to ask.
  10. AI and CognitiveThe race to see, decide, and act faster than an institution built for a slower age can answer.
  11. BiologicalSlow degradation through a deniable proxy chain, kept permanently below the line of acknowledged war.
  12. Data and SurveillanceThe substrate. What makes the other eleven accurate.

Why this book can be checked

Books in this territory tend to argue past their evidence. This one is built so a hostile reader can audit it.

Every part separates fact from argument

Each of the twenty components closes with a sourcing section that states three things plainly: which claims rest on primary sources, which are the book's inference and are labelled as such in the text, and which figures were narrowed or cut because the sourcing would not support them.

Contested numbers are stated conservatively

Twelve figures in general circulation appear here in narrower form, each recorded with what it would take to restore a number. A conservative figure that survives challenge is worth more than a striking one that does not.

Famous figures are checked for provenance

Where a well-known statistic turned out to have been famous as propaganda or as marketing, it is reframed and the reframing is stated. A book about information warfare cannot repeat an information operation as an engineering statistic.

The framework is turned on the United States too

The same apparatus is applied to an American institutional failure with no adversary participating at all, because a framework that only ever indicts the other side is not a framework.
