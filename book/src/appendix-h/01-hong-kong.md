### Hong Kong

**Primary documents.** Notes marked *New* enter in this edition. The rest carry forward from the first edition.

**\[1\]** Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. The law took effect at 11 p.m. local time on June 30, 2020. Article numbers appear in the text at each use. **Verified.** The full text appeared only at commencement, and the Legislative Council never voted on it.

**\[2\]** *New.* Hong Kong Free Press and the South China Morning Post reported Demosisto's dissolution on June 30, 2020. Studentlocalism and the Hong Kong National Front ended their Hong Kong operations the same day.

**\[3\]** *New.* The Associated Press and CNN reported the Hong Kong Police Force statements of July 1, 2020. The first arrest came shortly after midday. Police reported about 370 arrests that day, ten under the new law.

**\[4\]** *New.* Implementation Rules for Article 43, gazetted July 6, 2020. The rules cover content removal and data demands, interception on executive authorization and penalties for service providers who refuse. The Office for Safeguarding National Security opened on July 8, 2020. The police national security department dates from June 30, 2020.

**\[5\]** Apple Daily printed its final edition on June 24, 2021. Stand News closed on December 29, 2021, after a police raid and arrests. Citizen News announced its closure on January 2, 2022. Prosecutors charged the forty-seven on February 28, 2021, as the British government's six-monthly report for that half year records. Public libraries began withdrawing books for review in July 2020.

**\[6\]** Britain and the People's Republic signed the Joint Declaration on December 19, 1984, and registered it with the United Nations. The National People's Congress adopted the Basic Law in April 1990, and it took effect on July 1, 1997.

**\[7\]** Decision of the National People's Congress of May 28, 2020, on the legal system and enforcement mechanisms for safeguarding national security in Hong Kong. Decision of the National People's Congress of March 11, 2021, on improving the city's electoral system. **\[8\]** The Security Bureau froze HK$18 million of assets held by three Apple Daily companies on June 17, 2021. The sum equals about US$2.3 million. Company filings and Security Bureau statements carry the figure. **Verified.**

**\[9\]** Police arrested Jimmy Lai and searched Apple Daily on August 10, 2020. Police put the number of officers at about two hundred. Apple Daily streamed the search live on its Facebook page, as Global News and Nieman Lab reported.

**\[10\]** The Legislative Council passed the Safeguarding National Security Ordinance in March 2024.

**\[11\]** The Professional Teachers' Union and the Civil Human Rights Front dissolved in August 2021. The Hong Kong Alliance dissolved in September 2021, and its June 4th Museum had closed that June. The government's statement on the slogan dates from July 2, 2020. National security education began in schools in February 2021. The Court of Appeal granted the injunction against the protest anthem in May 2024.

**\[12\]** *New.* National security police demanded the 612 Humanitarian Relief Fund's operational records in 2021. The fund suspended donations in September 2021 after its trustee advised that the bank would no longer process payments from the fund's account.

**\[13\]** Article 38 of the law. The Hong Kong Police Force announced HK$1 million bounties in July and December 2023.

**\[14\]** *New.* Basic Law Articles 18 and 158 and Annex III. The Standing Committee added the National Security Law to Annex III on June 30, 2020, and the Chief Executive promulgated it the same day.

**\[15\]** *New.* The Foreign Secretary's six-monthly reports to Parliament on Hong Kong. The report for January to June 2021 records the declaration of March 13, 2021. Parliamentary briefing material and published scholarship on the treaty note its lack of an enforcement mechanism.

**\[16\]** *New.* Congress passed the Hong Kong Policy Act in 1992. The Human Rights and Democracy Act became law on November 27, 2019. The Autonomy Act became law on July 14, 2020. **\[18\]** *New.* The platforms announced their pauses on July 6 and 7, 2020. Contemporary reports in Forbes, Fortune and CNBC name them. TikTok withdrew from Hong Kong in the same week.

**Corrections to the first edition.** Five claims in the first edition did not survive a check against the record, and one framing needs narrowing.

*First arrests.* The first edition put them within the hour. The first arrest came shortly after midday on July 1, about thirteen hours after commencement. \[3\]

*Bank freezes.* The first edition said banks began freezing the accounts of designated organizations within four hours. No record supports the claim. The freezes documented here run from December 2020 through June 2021. \[8\] \[25\]

*App removals.* The first edition said protest apps left the local app store within eight hours. No record supports the claim. The record shows the rules of July 6, the platforms' pauses and TikTok's exit within the first week. \[4\] \[18\]

*Academic dismissals.* The first edition placed them in the first day. The University of Hong Kong's council dismissed Benny Tai on July 28, 2020, four weeks after commencement. \[28\]

*The forty-seven.* The first edition said the Party arrested them. Police arrested a larger group in January 2021, and prosecutors charged forty-seven of them in February. \[5\]

*One night.* The law's powers arrived in one night, and the dismantling took eighteen months. The Hong Kong section of *The Officer's Burden* repeats the first-arrest line and a forty-eight-hour framing, and both need the same correction.

**Stated as inference, not finding.**

*The coordination.* The record documents every step. The timeline supports one design behind them, and no document states it.

*The wider aim.* The Architecture Vision argues it from what the Party did next.

*The gap readings.* Every paragraph marked Argued in the Gap Analysis belongs here, and so does the principles audit.

*The platform analogy.* Gap 6 reads documented articles of two laws as a platform with administrator rights. The articles are fact and the analogy is the architect's.

*The target.* Every element of the target architecture is argument.

*The guarantee line.* The claim that a guarantee its guarantor alone can revoke is a timing decision stands as a judgment.

*The Taiwan connection.* The historical record documents Deng Xiaoping's design of One Country, Two Systems as a model for Taiwan. The operation's effect on Taipei follows from events. Whether the Party intended that signal remains open.

The first edition called the operation the most efficient dismantling of a free society in the modern record. The comparison is gone, because the timeline makes the point without it. The ten-dimension walk became the principles audit in the Gap Analysis, and all ten dimensions survive there. The Huawei comparison is gone, because the defenders' clock supplies Hong Kong's own evidence of sequential defense.
