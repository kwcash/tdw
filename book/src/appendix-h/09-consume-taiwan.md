**Primary documents.** Notes marked *New* enter in this edition. The rest carry forward from the first edition.

**\[1\]** The Republic of China's Ministry of National Defense published daily reports on the air-defense identification zone for October 1 to 4, 2021. **Verified.** The ministry counted 149 sorties across the four days, a record at the time.

**\[2\]** The PLA oath of enlistment and the Party's doctrine of absolute leadership over the armed forces. The preamble of the People's Republic's constitution and successive Party congress reports state the doctrine.

**\[3\]** Economic Cooperation Framework Agreement, signed June 29, 2010. **Scoped** as in the first edition, with the magnitude of trade stated and no point figure.

**\[5\]** Ministry of National Defense daily incursion series, 2023 annual compilation.

**\[6\]** *New.* Joint Sword-2024A ran on May 23 and 24, 2024. Joint Sword-2024B ran on October 14 and 15, 2024. Strait Thunder-2025A ran on April 1 and 2, 2025. Justice Mission-2025 ran on December 29 and 30, 2025. The Australian Strategic Policy Institute's tracker records the coast guard's first participation in May 2024. The Global Taiwan Institute's analyses record the stated triggers and the encirclement theme.

**\[7\]** *New.* The State Council's tariff commission announced the first suspension on December 21, 2023. It took effect on January 1, 2024. The commission announced the second on May 31, 2024, and it took effect on June 15. Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council protested the second the same day.

**\[8\]** *New.* United Nations General Assembly Resolution 2758, October 25, 1971. The resolution does not mention Taiwan.

**\[9\]** *New, updated through this edition's research.* Taiwan's prosecutors brought the charges on June 10, 2025. The four defendants were Huang Chu-jung, Ho Jen-chieh, Chiu Shih-yuan and Wu Shang-yu. The Taipei District Court convicted and sentenced all four on September 25, 2025. Terms ran from four to ten years, the heaviest against Huang for espionage, organizing a criminal network for China and money laundering. The Taiwan High Court ruled on appeal on June 25, 2026. It reduced Huang's sentence to six years, Chiu's to five and Wu's to three, and acquitted Ho outright. The court did not immediately publish its reasoning. The United States Indo-Pacific Command's legal update of June 13, 2025 recorded the original charges.

**\[10\]** *New.* The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency issued its joint advisory on Volt Typhoon on February 7, 2024.

**\[11\]** Taiwan FactCheck Center and Doublethink Lab documentation of synthetic media around the January 2024 presidential election.

**\[12\]** The Party's customs administration suspended Taiwanese pineapple imports in March 2021 and grouper imports in June 2022.

**\[13\]** Republic of China Ministry of Foreign Affairs record of diplomatic allies. **Updated.** Taiwan's foreign ministry confirmed twelve allies in December 2025.

**\[14\]** *New.* Taiwan Relations Act, April 10, 1979.

**\[15\]** *New.* Taiwan Security Monitor at George Mason University, arms sale backlog updates from June 2025 to January 2026. Its January 2026 update records the stalled special budget. Taiwan's defense ministry reports to the legislature supply the status of each case.

**\[16\]** *New.* Hesai's annual report on Form 20-F records its February 2023 offering and its designation of January 31, 2024. Hesai challenged the listing on May 13, 2024. Judge Paul L. Friedman of the District Court for the District of Columbia upheld it in July 2025. His opinion describes the bar on Defense contracting from 2026. Senators Rick Scott, Marsha Blackburn and Bernie Moreno wrote to the SEC and Treasury in September 2025. **Corrected.** The first edition implied the listing and the designation ran at the same time.

**\[17\]** TSMC financial disclosures and industry reporting. **Scoped** as in the first edition. The share refers to leading-edge logic, not to semiconductors generally. *New.* TSMC's first Arizona plant began volume production in late 2024.

**\[18\]** *New.* The administration notified Congress of more than $11 billion in sales on December 17, 2025, the largest package on record. The guided-bomb sale dates from 2017 and the F-16 Block 70/72 sale from 2019. Taiwan Security Monitor records the delays.

**\[19\]** Taiwan National Communications Commission reports on the Matsu cable damage, February 2023. **Attribution note** carried forward. Formal attribution in the individual incidents remains unresolved.

**\[20\]** *New.* The Hong Tai 58 cut the Taiwan and Penghu No. 3 cable on February 25, 2025. The court convicted its captain on June 12, 2025 and sentenced him to three years. The appeal failed. A later civil ruling awarded Chunghwa Telecom about NT$18.2 million, roughly $570,000.
