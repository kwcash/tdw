**Primary documents.** Notes marked *New* enter in this edition. The rest carry forward from the first edition.

**\[1\]** Justice Department releases describe Fujian Jinhua as a state-owned enterprise of the People's Republic. **Verified.** The Commerce Department's Entity List notice of October 29, 2018 states that Jinhua was completing a $5.7 billion production facility, the source of the funding figure. Reuters reporting from the same week gives the same figure.

**\[2\]** *New.* The Justice Department release of October 28, 2020 describes the two engineers and the Micron file used to adjust UMC's design rules.

**\[3\]** *New.* UMC's own filings with the SEC record the Taichung indictment of August 31, 2017 and Micron's civil suit of December 5, 2017. The Taichung District Court ruled on June 12, 2020. It fined UMC NT$100 million and sentenced three engineers to terms of four and a half to six and a half years. The retrial in January 2022 cut the fine to NT$20 million and acquitted one engineer. It reduced the other two sentences to six months and one year with probation. UMC's statement of October 2020 gives its account of the district court's findings. Micron's counsel took part in that trial, by UMC's account.

**\[4\]** *New.* UMC's SEC filings record that it filed its Fuzhou suits on January 12, 2018. Jinhua filed its own. The court granted the injunction on July 3, 2018. Micron's statement of July 5, 2018 records its objection that the court ruled before hearing its defense. UMC withdrew its Fuzhou complaints on November 26, 2021 under its settlement with Micron.

**\[5\]** Commerce Department Entity List addition for Fujian Jinhua, announced October 29, 2018. *New.* The Bureau of Industry and Security issued its semiconductor rules on October 7, 2022.

**\[6\]** Prosecutors filed the indictment in *United States v. United Microelectronics Corporation* under seal on September 27, 2018. The court unsealed it on November 1, 2018. *New.* The Attorney General announced the China Initiative that day. Assistant Attorney General Matthew Olsen ended it on February 23, 2022. Reporting at the time counted more than seventy prosecutions under it.

**\[7\]** UMC pleaded guilty on October 28, 2020 to one count under 18 U.S.C. § 1832(a)(3). It paid a $60 million fine and agreed to cooperate. **Corrected.** The first edition called the plea one to trade secret theft generally.

**\[8\]** Judge Maxine M. Chesney acquitted Fujian Jinhua after a bench trial on February 27, 2024. **Verified.** Micron and Jinhua settled in December 2023, and Micron and UMC settled in November 2021.

**\[9\]** *New.* The Cyberspace Administration of China opened its review on March 31, 2023 and announced its decision on May 21, 2023. The same report gave mainland revenue of $3.4 billion, or 12 percent of the total, and called Micron the first American chipmaker targeted. **\[13\]** *New.* The National Intellectual Property Administration issued its notice on patent application behavior on January 27, 2021. The Patent and Trademark Office published its study of non-market factors in mainland patent and trademark filings on January 13, 2021.

**\[15\]** *New.* The grand jury indicted Ding in March 2024 and returned a superseding indictment in February 2025. **\[16\]** Pat Choate prepared *A Great Wall of Patents* as a working paper for the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission on November 7, 2005. The electronics maker's account and the Japanese inquiry count are Choate's, drawn from Japanese press reporting. Both are secondary.

The America Invents Act of 2011 made the United States first-inventor-to-file for applications filed from March 16, 2013.

**\[18\]** *New.* Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act, signed December 18, 2020. The Public Company Accounting Oversight Board announced complete inspection access on December 15, 2022.

**Corrections to the first edition.** Four claims did not survive a check against the record, and one omission changed the case.

*The Fuzhou injunction.* The first edition placed it during the American prosecution and credited Jinhua alone. UMC and Jinhua filed in January 2018, and the court ruled on July 3, 2018. That was four months before any American government action. \[4\]

*The plea.* UMC pleaded guilty to receiving and possessing a stolen trade secret, one count. \[7\]

*The AI channel.* The first edition's note dropped the replication intervals, but its text kept them. It also claimed that model weights are often reconstructable from a paper, with no source. Both claims are gone. The Ding conviction carries the AI channel instead. \[15\]

*The case length.* The first edition's five and a half years ran from the sealed indictment of September 2018. This edition says more than five years from unsealing, which the record supports either way.

*The Taiwan prosecution.* The first edition omitted it. Taiwan indicted UMC first, convicted it first and cut the sentences on retrial. UMC's account of that ruling agrees with the American acquittal on the point that matters. Neither court found that the secret reached Jinhua. \[3\]

**A cross-case correction for *The Borrowed Knife*.** Its patent section says the eighteen-month rule came from implementing the World Trade Organization agreement. The rule came from the American Inventors Protection Act of 1999. Its remedies ask Congress to restore the publication exemption, and the exemption already exists for inventors who file only at home. Choate's patent material now lives here, and that section can shrink to a cross-reference when the case is rebuilt. \[17\]
