### Protocols and Ports

**Primary documents.** Notes marked *New* enter in this edition. The rest carry forward from the first edition, renumbered.

**\[1\]** *Verified for this edition.* The 3GPP TSG RAN1 #87 meeting convened in Reno, Nevada. It ran November 14 to 18, 2016, and selected polar codes as the control-channel coding scheme for 5G enhanced mobile broadband. The selection is documented across multiple 3GPP working papers and patent filings referencing the meeting by number and location.

**\[2\]** The House Committee on Homeland Security and the Select Committee on the CCP released their joint investigative report on September 12, 2024. The Wall Street Journal first reported the modem findings in March 2024. The nearly 80 percent crane-share figure and the ZPMC, COSCO and China Merchants ties come from the same report.

**\[3\]** The Central Committee and the State Council issued the National Standardization Development Outline on October 10, 2021.

**\[4\]** The European Telecommunications Standards Institute's register of declared essential patents, and industry counts of 5G declarations.

**\[5\]** *Verified for this edition.* The Wuhan Intermediate People's Court's order in the Xiaomi and InterDigital dispute, September 2020. The Chongqing court's rate ruling in the OPPO and Nokia dispute, December 2023. The European Union filed its WTO request for consultations on February 18, 2022, over China's anti-suit injunctions in standard-essential patent disputes. The EU requested a WTO panel on December 7, 2022 after consultations failed.

**\[6\]** *Verified for this edition.* Zhao Houlin served as ITU Secretary-General from 2015 to 2022. The ITU elected Doreen Bogdan-Martin at its Plenipotentiary Conference in Bucharest on September 29, 2022. She won 139 votes to 25 for Russia's Rashid Ismailov, out of 172 member states present and voting.

**\[7\]** *Verified for this edition.* Huawei and state-owned partners submitted the New IP proposal to the ITU-T's Telecommunications Standardization Advisory Group in September 2019. Reporting on the proposal and on facial-recognition standards proposals followed in 2019 and 2020. The ITU-T's Study Group 13 and Study Group 11 plenary declined to accept New IP as a new work item in December 2020.

**\[8\]** The Trade Representative issued its Section 301 finding on January 16, 2025. Its fees took effect on October 14, 2025, the same day as the Party's Ministry of Transport fees on American-linked ships.

**\[9\]** The U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission published its staff brief on LOGINK on September 20, 2022.

**\[10\]** Commerce issued its general advisory opinion on August 19, 2019. It issued interim final rules in June 2020, September 2022 and July 2024. The 2022 rule's notice records the effect of the uncertainty on American participation.

**\[11\]** The defense act for fiscal 2024 carries the LOGINK prohibitions, effective six months after enactment.

**\[12\]** The White House fact sheet of November 1, 2025 announced the suspension of three instruments. The Section 301 port fees, the Commerce affiliates rule and the Party's rare-earth export rule all became effective November 10, 2025. All three are due to expire around November 10, 2026 absent further action.

**\[13\]** *New, updated through this edition's research.* CK Hutchison announced its agreement to sell its global port portfolio on March 4, 2025, naming the Panama Ports Company among the assets and valuing the transaction at $22.8 billion. China's State Administration for Market Regulation opened an antitrust review. Chinese state media and officials pressed for COSCO Shipping to take a stake, later reported as a demand for a controlling rather than minority position. Panama's Supreme Court ruled CK Hutchison's underlying concession for the Balboa and Cristóbal terminals unconstitutional, in a decision reported from late February 2026, removing the Panama terminals from the transaction. MSC's Terminal Investment Limited took over interim operation of the Cristóbal terminal under an eighteen-month contract. Reporting on the remaining forty-one ports shows negotiations continuing without a signed agreement, unresolved over Chinese state-carrier participation. A Financial Times report from early March 2026 and a trade-press report from September 7, 2026 both confirm the standoff. **This item should be checked again at final proof**, per this brief's own instruction on any claim stated as current.

**\[14\]** The White House issued the United States Government National Standards Strategy for Critical and Emerging Technology on May 4, 2023. Its implementation roadmap followed in July 2024.

**\[15\]** *New.* The citation is to Executive Order 14028, signed May 12, 2021. Its formal title is "Improving the Nation's Cybersecurity." Section 4(e) directs the NIST guidance that led to the software bill of materials requirement for federal software suppliers.

**Corrections and changes from the first edition.**

*The domain list.* Both source chapters' headers now use the fixed domain names in their fixed order, merged into one list.

*The merge.* This edition merges two first-edition chapters, *The Rulebook* and *Ports and Cranes*, into one Technology Architecture case for this edition. It reads standards as the logical layer and ports and cranes as the physical layer. Both signature gaps are kept. One sits at Architecture Principles, where export controls kept American engineers out of standards meetings for five years. The other sits at Technology, where the cranes carried a channel nobody requested. Both guardrails are kept too. No contribution is suspect because of its source, and crane workers are not agents.

*The Panama item.* The first edition's Ports and Cranes chapter described the CK Hutchison sale as pending Panama's approval. This edition updates the record through the Panama Supreme Court ruling and the ongoing renegotiation of the non-Panama portfolio, and flags the item as needing a final check before print given how quickly it continues to move.

**Stated as inference, not finding.**

*The coordination, at both layers.* The record documents the national outline and the state enterprises' ownership. Reading a single coordinated design behind the standards delegations and the port enterprises alike is the framework's argument.

*The chain across altitudes.* That the same six gaps recur at the logical and physical layers is this chapter's organizing claim, not a documented finding.

*The gap readings.* Every paragraph marked Argued in the Gap Analysis belongs here, and so does the principles audit.

*The target.* Every element of the target architecture is argument.

**The guardrails that must survive any edit.** No contribution to a standards body is suspect because of its source, and a good standard is good engineering wherever it comes from. Crane workers, welders and engineers build excellent cranes at low prices, and none of them are agents. The case concerns the enterprises and the Party's strategy for them, never the people who staff them.
