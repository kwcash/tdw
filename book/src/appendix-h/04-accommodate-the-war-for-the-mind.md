**Corrections and changes from the first edition.**

*The limits.* The first edition said the export version carries no limits. TikTok set a sixty-minute default for minors in March 2023. The contrast now rests on enforcement, a hard limit tied to real-name registration against a soft default.

*The intent language.* The first edition's opening said ByteDance engineered the export app to hold attention at any cost, and it called the contrast a confession. The sourcing note had already disclaimed that intent. The opening now matches the note.

*The deal.* The first edition described the divestiture. This edition adds the Party's export-control veto, the licensing of the algorithm and the Party's approval.

*The domain list.* The header now uses the fixed domain names in their fixed order.

**Removed and kept removed.** The first edition removed a widely shared comparison of Chinese and Western children's career ambitions, which traced to a single commercial survey with mismatched samples. It stays out.

**Stated as inference, not finding.**

*Design intent.* No public document shows engineers tuning either the platform or a specific campaign to degrade a rival population, as distinct from tuning for engagement or running a lawful operation with degradation as a byproduct. The strategic usefulness of the effect is the framework's argument.

*The Digital Twin.* The predictive model of a population's divisions follows from how recommendation systems work. Its use by the Party's networks, and by its target audience analysts, is the framework's argument.

*The objective function as governance.* The record documents the registry and the regulations. The reading of that record as a governance decision is the framework's.

*Whether one office runs both halves.* No public document shows whether the platform's objective function and the practitioner's target audience analysis sit under one office or separate arms of the same authority. This chapter's argument does not depend on which is true.
