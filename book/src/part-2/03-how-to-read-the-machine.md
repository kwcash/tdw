## How to Read the Machine

*A Field Card Before the Cases Start*

The Stack explained the architecture. This chapter is shorter, and it exists for one purpose. Every case chapter from here forward uses the same four vocabularies and the same five-step reading order, and a reader who has them in hand before Part III starts will not need to flip back for them.

### The Four Vocabularies, in Brief

**Ten dimensions.** Time. Entropy. Topology. Asymmetry. Agency. Resonance. Phase. Gradient. Criticality. Coherence. These are properties of a contest, not places within it, and they decide whether an actor's advantages multiply or cancel. Coherence is the master dimension. The full table, with the question each dimension asks and the principle it sets, lives in Appendix C.

**Twelve domains.** Kinetic. Economic. Financial. Information. Psychological. Legal. Association. Cyber. Cultural. AI and Cognitive. Biological. Data and Surveillance. These are the arenas where the dimensions become concrete, and every case names which domains it uses. The full table lives in Appendix C alongside the dimensions.

**Thirty-six stratagems.** A finite catalog of techniques sorted into six groups by the position an actor holds when it reaches for one, from Superiority through Desperation. A case names the stratagem it finds at work the way a security team names a technique from an attack framework, not as a literary flourish. The full catalog, with the Taiwan case mapped against it in full, lives in Appendix B.

**Five views.** Every case runs the same order. Architecture Vision states what the Party's enterprise set out to achieve. The Party's baseline records what it fielded, layer by layer. The defenders' baseline records the same layers on the side under attack, with an owner named for each. Gap analysis locates every seam the campaign used. Target architecture states what would close each gap and what it would cost. A reader who loses the thread in the middle of a case can always ask which of these five sections they are in.

### A Note for the Military Reader

This framework is not a competitor to your doctrine, and it does not ask you to set your own vocabulary aside. The ten dimensions map onto the nine principles of war and onto joint doctrine's own functions, and Appendix D lays out that crosswalk in full. One asymmetry in the doctrine itself is worth knowing before you get there. Joint doctrine added information as a seventh joint function in 2017. The Army's own warfighting functions, descended from the six that replaced the old Battlefield Operating Systems in 2008, have not followed suit and still number six. Where your own service's doctrine and the joint level disagree with each other, this framework's crosswalk in Appendix D notes the disagreement rather than picking a side.

### Where This Leaves the Reader

None of the four vocabularies above does any work on its own. A dimension without a domain is an abstraction. A domain without a stratagem is an empty arena. A stratagem without a target architecture is a description with no fix attached. The cases ahead put all four to work together, case by case, and the five-view order is what holds them in a shape a reader can follow from one chapter to the next.

Part III begins the cases proper.
