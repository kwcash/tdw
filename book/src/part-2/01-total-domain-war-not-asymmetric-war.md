## Total Domain War, Not Asymmetric War

*The West Filed the Method Under the Wrong Heading*

On April 15, 2014, Xi Jinping presided over the first meeting of a commission that had not existed eighteen months earlier. \[1\] The Central National Security Commission had one item on its agenda that mattered beyond the room. Xi introduced a concept he called the overall national security outlook, in Chinese *zongti guojia anquan guan*. *Zongti* can also be rendered total. \[1\]

The word choice was not incidental. The rest of this chapter reads it as a claim about method, not a slogan.

### The Western Frame

For most of the past three decades the West has filed the Party's methods under asymmetric warfare, hybrid warfare, or the gray zone. Each label assumes the same shape underneath it. A weaker side, unable to win a direct contest, reaches for tricks at the margins.

That assumption rests on real scholarship and answers a real question. Ivan Arreguín-Toft's *How the Weak Win Wars* (2005) built a rigorous account of how materially inferior actors defeat stronger ones, and the asymmetry dimension in this framework's own model owes that literature a debt. \[2\] The question the asymmetry literature answers is how the weak beat the strong, a real question with a real answer. Nothing in this chapter argues otherwise.

*Unrestricted Warfare* fits the same frame, and the frame has misled a generation of its Western readers. Qiao Liang and Wang Xiangsui published the book in 1999 as two PLA colonels answering a question their own military faced directly. Beijing had watched the Gulf War of 1991 and concluded that no force on earth could out-fight the American military machine in a straight contest. \[3\] The book that followed reads, correctly, as a weaker power's manual for winning without fighting that contest. Western reviewers filed it as an asymmetric warfare text, and for the purpose its authors wrote it, the filing was accurate.

The error is assuming the frame that fit a weaker power in 1999 still fits the party that runs the campaign today.

### The Party's Own Frame Is Totality

Four documents, read together, show the shift.

**Documented.** On April 15, 2014, Xi Jinping proposed the overall national security outlook at the first meeting of the Central National Security Commission. \[1\] The concept named political and military security, economic and cultural security, and scientific security as one connected problem. The list of named fields has grown since. \[1\] The National Security Law of July 2015 codified the outlook in statute. Article 11 assigns every citizen, every state body, every enterprise and every social organization in the country a duty to safeguard national security. \[4\] The Party's own constitution, amended at the 19th Congress in October 2017, states that the Party exercises overall leadership over every area of endeavor in every part of the country. \[5\] Freedom House's 2021 case study on transnational repression found that the Party runs the most sophisticated, global and comprehensive campaign of its kind anywhere in the world. \[6\]

**Argued.** Read apart, each document records a policy choice inside its own field. Read together, they describe one design decision applied everywhere at once. A security concept that names political and military fields, economic and cultural fields, and scientific fields in a single breath does not leave a field outside its reach to discover later. A constitution that claims leadership over every area of endeavor carves out no exception for finance, or standards, or a patent office. The Party did not arrive at totality by accumulating decisions one domain at a time, the way the record shows the West accumulating its own Enclosure. It named the totality first, in 2014, and has worked outward from the name since.

### The Framework Already Encodes It

A reader who has reached this chapter with the rest of the framework in hand has already met this argument once. Asymmetry is one dimension of ten, not the whole physics of the contest. Coherence is the tenth dimension and the master dimension, because it decides whether the other nine multiply or cancel. A total system running under one aim beats a divided system running nine separate aims, even where the divided side holds more resources in every domain measured separately. The model already says what the Party's own documents say about themselves. What this chapter corrects is the reader's frame, not the model.

### Total War Is Not New, and It Is Not Chinese

Erich Ludendorff named total war in 1935, in a book written by a general who had run Germany's own war economy two decades earlier and concluded that the next war would leave no civilian institution untouched. \[7\] The United States then ran total war for real, from 1941 to 1945. It fused industry and science, finance and information, and culture and arms into a single instrument that out-produced the Axis powers combined in munitions. Total war belongs to no single nation and no single century.

What is new is total war run in peacetime, across civilian domains, by a party that answers to no electorate and no court. Robert Jay Lifton spent years after the Korean War interviewing people who had passed through Chinese thought reform, and in 1961 he published eight criteria describing what he found in them. Milieu control. Mystical manipulation. The demand for purity. The cult of confession. Sacred science. The loading of the language. Doctrine placed over the person. The dispensing of existence itself to those the group casts out. \[8\] Lifton wrote that checklist to describe what a totalist system does to one mind at a time. The chapters ahead describe what the same design does to twelve domains at once.

Call it, once, what it is. Total totalitarian totality carries no alliteration for its own sake. It stacks three separate documented facts on top of each other, and this chapter has now supplied all three.

### The Reader's Redesign

A total system beats a divided one on first contact, and keeps beating it for as long as the divided side stays divided. Nothing about that outcome passes judgment on the divided side's people, its wealth or its ideas. The judgment falls on its wiring.

The chapters ahead trace that wiring failing in one domain at a time. The pages after that price out what rewiring it would cost. A total system defeats a divided one, and the fix available to the divided side has never been to become total in return. The fix is to stop being divided.
