## Accommodate

*The War for the Mind*

**DOMAINS** Economic · Information · **Psychological (spine)** · Legal · Association · Cyber · Cultural · **AI and Cognitive (spine)** · Data and Surveillance

**DIMENSIONS** Time · Entropy · Topology · Asymmetry · **Agency (signature)** · Resonance · Phase · Gradient · Criticality · Coherence

**STRATAGEMS** Spine S3 · S7 · S10 · S20 · S33 · S35

**GAPS** Seven. Two at Governance, one at each other layer. Signature gaps sit at Governance. No owner of the objective function, and the home audience has no owner.

ByteDance runs two versions of the same application. Douyin runs on the mainland. TikTok runs everywhere else.

Since 2021 Douyin's youth mode has limited users under fourteen to forty minutes a day and shut them out between ten at night and six in the morning, enforced through real-name registration tied to a national identity system. In March 2023 TikTok gave accounts under eighteen a default limit of sixty minutes. A teenager can extend it with a passcode or switch it off entirely, and nothing on the platform verifies who holds the phone. \[1\]

The difference lies in the layer that enforces the limit, not the limit itself. On the mainland the state's identity system makes it hard to cheat. Abroad it is a default a fourteen-year-old can dismiss in under a minute. The same identity system that protects the mainland child also tracks every mainland user's every action, and the protection comes bundled with the surveillance as one design, not two.

In a recommendation system the objective function decides what the machine optimizes for, whether time on the app, a curated diet of content or anything else an owner chooses to reward. Whoever sets that parameter governs a population's attention at the level the population itself never sees. The Party sets it for its own population through a regulator that requires every recommendation algorithm to register and publishes the registry. No one on the defenders' side owns the equivalent question at all, for any platform, foreign or domestic.

That is the platform half of this case. The practitioner half sits one layer up. Once a platform can steer attention, a state can pair it with covert accounts and human operators to study a chosen audience and move it toward a specific end. This chapter reads both halves as one campaign against one target, a population's capacity to decide.

### The Terms of This Case

The actor is the Party, not China and not the Chinese people. ByteDance is a company on the mainland, and the National Intelligence Law of 2017 obliges it to assist the security organs on demand. \[2\] The Party runs cognitive control on its own population too. Douyin is not freedom. The same machine runs there at a different setting, censored and surveilled and steered toward the values the Party approves. Both populations are targets.

Two disciplines meet in this chapter, and neither is persuasion in the ordinary sense. Persuasion engages reason in the open and leaves judgment intact, whichever way it lands. The platform discipline works differently. It degrades a whole society's shared sense of what is real, not by arguing against any particular fact but by making every fact compete for attention on the same terms as everything else, until the population cannot assemble the consensus it needs to act together on anything. The practitioner discipline is narrower and sharper than the platform, and it does not need the platform to exist, though it profits when the platform has already done its work. It selects a target audience, studies its vulnerabilities and its susceptibility to a given line, and shapes that audience's emotions and behavior toward an objective it may never see or name. Democracies run the narrower discipline too, lawfully and under approval chains and doctrine, aimed abroad rather than at their own people. This chapter measures architecture on both sides of both disciplines, not the morality of the tool itself.

Two guardrails hold for everything that follows. Nothing in the target architecture polices the lawful speech of the defenders' own citizens. And where this chapter names a routine, it names the routine, not the person exposed to it. Labels and disclosure are the remedy throughout. Removal of lawful speech is never the remedy.

One boundary limits both halves. No public document shows an engineer tuning a recommendation system to degrade a rival population as distinct from tuning it for engagement with degradation as a byproduct, and no public document describes the Party's analysts at work on a given campaign. The case argues that the effect serves the Party whether or not anyone designed it to, and that operations too precise in timing to be coincidence imply analysis behind them. Readers should hold both as arguments.

A further question follows naturally from merging these two halves into one chapter, and it deserves a direct answer. No public document shows whether one office runs both the platform's objective function and the practitioner's target audience analysis, or whether they sit in separate arms that answer to the same authority without coordinating day to day. The management plane the *Military-Civil Fusion* case describes would make either structure possible under one command. This case does not depend on which one is true, because a defender facing either structure sees the same absence of an owner on its own side.

### 1. Architecture Vision

**Documented.** The Party's cyberspace regulator requires platforms to register their recommendation algorithms and publishes a registry of them. It drafted rules in 2023 to set daily limits for minors across mobile apps by age. The People's Liberation Army wrote public-opinion, psychological and legal warfare into its political-work regulations in 2003. PLA writers describe the mind itself as a domain of war in their own doctrine, cognitive domain operations. \[3\] \[4\] \[5\]

**Argued.** The Party governs attention and runs target audience analysis on whole populations, at home and abroad, faster than any defender can see the operation. At home the objective is stability, held through a registry no outside body audits. Abroad it is division, doubt and the belief that resistance is futile, pursued through channels the Party's own citizens cannot legally reach in return.

### 2. The Party's Baseline

#### The Platform

The AI and Cognitive domain carries this half, because the recommendation engine is both the delivery system and the aiming mechanism.

**Economic.** The advertising and creator economy funds the reach and deepens the dependence.

**Information.** Covert networks amplify, suppress and fabricate. In August 2023 Meta removed 7,704 Facebook accounts and hundreds of pages and groups in the largest covert influence operation it had found, a network researchers call Spamouflage. Meta linked it to individuals associated with the Party's law enforcement. \[6\]

**Psychological.** The engagement engine rewards short-cycle stimulation and outrage. A growing body of research links heavy short-form video use to effects on adolescent attention and emotional regulation. The literature is young and contested. \[7\]

**Legal.** The National Intelligence Law makes the platform's parent compellable. In August 2020 the Party added recommendation technology to its export-control catalog. \[2\] \[8\]

**Cultural.** The contrast between the two configurations is its own evidence of what the builders believe about their product.

**AI and Cognitive.** The Party's networks now use generative AI. OpenAI reported in May 2024 that Spamouflage had used its models to write and translate content. \[9\]

**Data and Surveillance.** TikTok told Congress it served about 170 million American users. Their behavior was the raw material of the model. \[10\]

A recommendation engine builds a predictive model of each user from behavior, and aggregated across a population the model shows which divisions carry the most charge, information no census or poll could gather at the same resolution or speed. The Party's covert networks aim at those divisions and amplify the most polarizing voices on several sides at once, rather than picking one side and pushing only it. A society under that pressure experiences its own fragmentation as an internal failure, because the grievances are real and domestic even where the amplification behind them is not, which is precisely what makes the operation hard to name while it is happening.

No public document shows the Party drawing on TikTok's American data to aim its networks abroad. The model's existence follows from how recommendation systems work anywhere. Its use by the Party for this purpose is the framework's argument, not a documented fact.

**Stratagem 10, Hide a Knife Behind a Smile**, is the platform's whole design. The degradation arrives inside delightful entertainment, never announced and never felt as an attack by the person experiencing it. **Stratagem 33, Sow Discord Among the Enemy**, amplifies a society's fault lines rather than inventing new ones. **Stratagem 7, Create Something from Nothing**, runs the synthetic personas and coordinated networks of the Spamouflage type. They manufacture apparent consensus where none exists. **Stratagem 20, Muddy the Waters to Catch the Fish**, harvests advantage from a population that can no longer tell signal from noise. The other three stratagems together help produce exactly that condition.

The platform is the recommendation engine and the cloud it runs on. The Party's export-control listing turned the algorithm into controlled technology, so any sale of TikTok's American business needed the Party's consent, granted only after five years of American effort. \[8\] \[11\]

#### The Practitioner

American doctrine runs psychological operations as a process, and the process reads cleanly as an enterprise architecture. Its core is target audience analysis. It identifies an audience and the conditions it lives under. Then it rates the audience's vulnerability, its susceptibility to a given line, and the channels that reach it. \[22\]

Target audience analysis is a data problem before it is anything else. The practitioner needs to know what an audience fears, resents and wants, which messages it will accept and which channels it trusts. The Party holds that data on its own population through its platforms, its dossiers and its real-name registration. Abroad it holds what the open record, breaches and the platforms themselves supply, including the recommendation data the platform half of this case already describes. A practitioner working from that depth of data can rate vulnerability, susceptibility and accessibility for a population segment in hours rather than months. No public document shows the Party's analysts at that work. The precision of the timing and themes in the cases below argues for the analysis behind them, without proving it.

| Process step | Architecture layer | The Party's version | The defenders' version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target audience analysis | Data | Runs on the harvest the *Digital Twin* case describes | Runs on smaller, mostly foreign data sets |
| Product development | Application | Human teams and AI output at scale | Trained teams working product by product |
| Dissemination | Technology | Its own platforms at home, open platforms abroad | Foreign channels only, by law and policy |
| Approval | Governance | One authority, through the management plane | Approval chains that doctrine requires |
| Assessment | Feedback loop | Platform metrics read in near real time | Criteria set in advance and measured later |

**Legal.** Legal warfare is one of the three warfares by the PLA's own regulations, and the Anti-Secession Law of 2005 serves as a standing psychological instrument as well as a legal one, since its terms are read aloud to a Taiwanese audience every time tensions rise rather than left to sit unused in a statute book.

**Association.** United-front networks and trusted local voices carry messages in communities that would discount the same message from an official source, whether or not the carrier knows where the message began.

**Cyber.** Intrusion supplies private material that operations later leak or twist, turning a breach into a talking point months or years after the breach itself.

**Cultural.** Identity operations recast self-government as betrayal of a shared heritage, as the *Taiwan* case records in its own detail.

The PLA's political officers own the three warfares by regulation, not by informal practice, which means a commander who wants to run one has a chain of command to answer to rather than a norm to interpret. The Propaganda Department sets the line for what the domestic public hears. The Cyberspace Administration works as one institution with two nameplates, the office of the Party's cyberspace commission and a state agency, so the same desk that drafts an algorithm rule can draft a censorship order. Analysts identify Base 311 in Fuzhou as a PLA unit devoted to the three warfares against Taiwan specifically, a standing organization rather than an ad hoc task force stood up for one campaign and dissolved after. \[3\] \[5\]

**Stratagem 3, Kill with a Borrowed Knife**, uses the target's own users and grievances to do the damage. **Stratagem 35, Chain the Stratagems**, links both halves together. Data feeds the model, and the model builds the target audience analysis. The analysis produces the product, and the platform or the covert network disseminates it. The record documents each link. The chain is the framework's reading.

**Entropy.** The machine enforces order at home and exports disorder abroad. A regulator that can silence a rumor on the mainland in hours lets its networks seed a hundred contradictory rumors abroad and never has to clean up either mess.

**Resonance.** The practitioner half amplifies divisions the audience already carries rather than manufacturing new ones, which is why the operations below read as domestic even where the amplification is not.

**Coherence.** One authority governs both halves, the domestic feed's objective function and the operations run through it and around it. The record documents the registry, the regulations and the operations. The single design behind them is the framework's argument.

### 3. The Defenders' Baseline

No single enterprise defends the defenders' attention or their audiences. Congress and courts, the military and civilian agencies, and platforms each hold a piece.

#### The Platform

| Capability | Defender owner | Instrument |
|---|---|---|
| Foreign ownership of platforms | Congress and the President | The divest-or-ban law of 2024 |
| Children's data | Federal Trade Commission and Justice Department | The children's online privacy law |
| Platform design for minors | States, and Congress in pending bills | State design codes, many enjoined in court |
| Recommender transparency | No federal owner | None |

The European Union holds the one broad instrument among the defenders' allies. Its Digital Services Act requires the largest platforms to assess systemic risks and offer a feed that does not rely on profiling, and the European Commission opened proceedings against TikTok in February 2024 over minors' protection and addictive design. \[12\] No comparable statute exists in American law, so the comparison itself is part of the gap. The ally with the broader instrument is not the one whose citizens the operation is documented to have targeted most directly.

**The TikTok clock**

| Date | Owner | Routine | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| August 6, 2020 | President | Executive order against TikTok | Courts blocked it |
| August 28, 2020 | The Party | Recommendation technology added to export controls | A veto over any sale |
| April 24, 2024 | Congress | Divest-or-ban law | Upheld by the Supreme Court, January 17, 2025 |
| August 2, 2024 | Justice Department | Suit alleging children's privacy violations | Pending in court |
| January 22, 2026 | Investors and both governments | Joint venture closes | ByteDance keeps 19.9 percent, algorithm licensed and retrained |

The Party inserted its export-control veto twenty-two days after the first American order, a response measured in weeks against a divestiture measured in years. The joint venture that finally closed did not remove the algorithm from ByteDance's reach. It licensed the algorithm, kept ByteDance as a minority owner, and retrained the model on American data under an outside auditor's watch rather than starting the model over from nothing. \[8\] \[11\] \[13\]

The joint venture stores American data in Oracle's cloud, retrains the algorithm on American data under Oracle's audit, and licenses the underlying model from ByteDance. A seven-member board with an American majority governs it. \[11\] The First Amendment limits how far government can shape what any platform shows, and courts have enjoined several state design codes. Those limits protect a free society from its own government. They also leave the objective function with no public owner.

#### The Practitioner

| Capability | Defender owner | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Psychological operations abroad | Military psychological operations units | Foreign audiences only, by law and policy |
| Truthful broadcasting into closed societies | Voice of America and Radio Free Asia | Cut back by executive order in 2025, litigated since |
| Exposing foreign propaganda | The Global Engagement Center | Lapsed in December 2024 |
| Detecting covert networks at home | Platforms and outside researchers | Voluntary |
| Resilience of the home audience | No owner | None |

The one operation the public record shows in detail is the Philippines campaign, and it went wrong. It ran from spring 2020 to mid-2021 through at least 300 fake accounts posing as Filipinos, aimed at undermining trust in a Chinese-made COVID vaccine in a partner nation still fighting the pandemic. Reuters reported that it began as payback for the Party's own effort to blame the United States for the pandemic's origin. The Defense Department did not deny the report and said it runs operations to counter adversary influence. \[14\] Whatever its provocation, a campaign that targets public health information during a pandemic spends a kind of credibility no later operation can buy back.

The truthful channel into the Party's own audience shrank during the period this edition covers. An executive order of March 14, 2025 directed the Agency for Global Media to cut to its statutory minimum, and staff across Voice of America and Radio Free Asia were placed on leave within days of the order. A federal judge ordered restoration in April, and an appeals court stayed parts of that order in May. The judge then blocked further cuts that September, leaving the broadcasters in a legal limbo that outlasted this edition's research. Supporters of the cuts called the broadcasters wasteful and politically biased. Press-freedom groups called the silence a gift to closed regimes that no other American institution could replace on short notice. \[15\]

At home the defenders run no counter-routine, by design. Their military may not target their own citizens, and their government may not police lawful speech. Those limits protect a free society from its own state. They also leave the home audience, where the Party's operations land, with no owner at all.

### 4. Why It Works: The Prepared Ground

Neither half of this case manufactures division. Both find division and widen it.

The weapon needs a population already losing its shared sense of what is true, sorted into distrustful camps and unsure whether its own institutions of knowledge tell it straight. Some of that erosion is organic, the fragmentation of a common media environment into private feeds that no platform designed on purpose. Some of it is self-inflicted. Serious people disagree over what schools should teach, over identity and classification in education, and over the institutions that run the schools. This framework takes no side in that argument. It observes only that the fight itself, and the distrust it breeds on every side, is the fault line both the platform and the practitioner seek.

The pandemic years deepened the distrust, as the *COVID-19* case records in detail. The Party built the platform half of this vulnerability and runs the practitioner half against it. The defenders own the ground half, and nothing in this chapter's target architecture asks them to surrender it.

Neither half needs the other to function, and each was drafted and documented separately before this edition merged them. Together they compound in a way neither half shows alone. The platform's recommendation data show which divisions in a population carry the most charge, the same divisions a target audience analysis needs to rate for vulnerability and susceptibility. Where the Party's own citizens are the audience, the platform and the practitioner can sit inside the same regulatory structure and never need to share data formally, because one authority already holds both. Where a foreign audience is the target, the platform supplies the terrain and the covert network supplies the message, and a population that has spent years inside an engagement-optimized feed arrives at any operation already sorted into the camps the operation will then amplify.

The compounding runs in the other direction too. A population trained by years of algorithmic feed to expect content tailored to what it already believes has a harder time recognizing a tailored operation as tailored. The platform does not need to coordinate with any specific campaign to make every later campaign easier. It only has to keep running.

### 5. A Target Audience Note

Both disciplines converge here, so this note carries the case's central illustration. The Taiwan electorate of 2024 serves as the example.

**Documented.** Party-linked actors used AI-generated audio, video and memes around the January 2024 vote, material realistic enough that fact-checkers had to build new verification workflows to keep pace with the volume. \[24\] The Party timed trade suspensions and military exercises to the election and the inauguration that followed it, so the message arrived through policy as well as through media. \[16\]

**Argued.** A target audience analysis would read the audience as younger and swing voters specifically, not the electorate as a whole. Their vulnerabilities would include housing costs, stagnant wages and a genuine fear of war that polling shows runs higher among younger cohorts than older ones. The line of persuasion would hold that America will not come when it matters and that resistance brings ruin without benefit. Susceptibility would run highest where economic worry meets distrust of the governing party, a combination that does not require any sympathy for the Party to produce. Accessibility would run through short-video apps, messaging groups and trusted local figures rather than through official news, which this audience already discounts. The Party's documented operations fit that analysis closely enough that the fit itself is evidence. Whether its own analysts wrote the analysis down in those terms, or simply acted on instinct built from decades of practice, is unknown.

This is not a new discipline. Soviet military theorists spent decades formalizing reflexive control, the practice of feeding an opponent selected information so that the opponent's own reasoning delivers the decision the sender wanted. Timothy Thomas's standard account of the theory, published in 2004, describes exactly the logic a modern target audience analysis automates. \[17\]

This is not a new practice either. In 1218 a Mongol trade caravan was massacred at Otrar, on the edge of the Khwarezmian Empire, and Genghis Khan's envoys carried his answer to every city that lay in his path afterward. Submit, and live under his rule. Resist, and be destroyed to the last name. \[18\] The message was tailored to each city's fear before a single soldier arrived, and it did as much of the Mongols' work as their cavalry.

### 6. Gap Analysis

**The principles audit**

| Principle | The Party's design | The defenders' design |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | Protects its children's formation on a generational horizon and runs operations continuously across decades | Left minors' design to defaults and pending bills, and funds counter-propaganda offices that lapse |
| **Entropy** | Order at home, disorder abroad | Absorbed the disorder, split across many owners |
| **Topology** | One registry over every domestic algorithm and every home channel | No registry, no owner, channels held by private firms |
| **Asymmetry** | Ran its apps abroad while it blocked foreign platforms at home | Kept its own market and channels open |
| **Agency** | Governs its population's feed and aims at audiences' capacity to decide | Left the feed to engagement metrics and the home audience's resilience to chance |
| **Resonance** | Covert networks amplify divisions already running hot | Divisions already running hot, unaddressed |
| **Phase** | Inserted a veto in twenty-two days, produces AI output at machine speed | Took five and a half years to divest one app, approves products one at a time |
| **Gradient** | A slope toward compulsive use abroad, fabricated consensus at home | Soft defaults a teenager can dismiss |
| **Criticality** | Aims at adolescent minds, elections and shared facts | Protected none of the three by design |
| **Coherence** | Governed by one authority through the management plane | No owner of the objective function or the home audience |

The cells summarize documented acts. The audit itself is the framework's reading.

#### Gap 1. No Owner of the Objective Function

*Architecture Governance. Signature gap, platform half*

**Documented.** The Party's regulator registers recommendation algorithms and drafted age limits for minors across apps. The United States has no federal registry of recommender objectives and no enacted federal design standard for minors, though several have been proposed. The Senate passed the Kids Online Safety Act in July 2024, and the House did not take it up. \[3\] \[4\] \[19\]

**Argued.** The objective function is a governance decision disguised as an engineering parameter, and disguising it costs nothing extra to build. The Party governs it at home through a named regulator with a public registry. In the United States the platform's revenue model governs it by default, whoever owns the platform, because no statute assigns the question to anyone. The divestiture addressed one company and left the mechanism untouched everywhere else it runs. Engagement-maximizing short-form video operates on platforms under many owners today, and the next instance of this gap need not announce its parentage the way TikTok's did.

#### Gap 2. The Home Audience Has No Owner

*Architecture Governance. Signature gap, practitioner half*

**Documented.** American psychological operations units may not target American audiences, by law and by the doctrine that trains them. The Global Engagement Center lapsed in 2024 without a statutory successor. \[23\] Platforms detect covert networks voluntarily, under no obligation to look or to report what they find. A 2022 study in *Science Advances* found that short inoculation videos shown as ads improved viewers' recognition of manipulation techniques on later exposure, and no public owner runs that kind of prebunking at any meaningful scale. \[20\]

**Argued.** The Party's operations land in the space where the defenders' state rightly declines to act, and that space has stayed empty rather than being filled by anyone else. The limits on the military and civilian agencies are not the defect. The absence of any owner for the audience's own resilience is the defect, built to work without policing a single word anyone says, and it has sat unaddressed since long before this edition's research began.

#### Gap 3. The Party Moves in Weeks, the Defenders in Years

*Architecture Principles, Phase*

**Documented.** The Party's export-control veto followed the first American order by twenty-two days. The joint venture closed five and a half years after that order. \[8\] \[11\] \[13\] Doctrine requires the defenders' psychological products to pass approval one at a time before release abroad. \[22\]

**Argued.** The Party answers in weeks on both halves of this case, with a veto and with AI output at machine speed. The defenders answer in years with a sale the Party approved, and with a chain that cannot answer an operation producing thousands of items an hour. Approval protects against error and abuse, and the Philippines campaign below shows exactly why that protection matters. But a chain that reviews every product one at a time cannot also match machine speed. Pre-approved themes and objectives, set in advance and audited afterward rather than cleared beforehand, would answer the speed without dropping the gate that keeps the defenders honest.

#### Gap 4. The Party's Data Advantage Runs Through Both Halves

*Data Architecture*

**Documented.** The TikTok joint venture moved American data into Oracle's cloud and retrains the algorithm on American data, but licenses the underlying model from ByteDance, which keeps 19.9 percent of the venture. The Party holds its own target audience data at home and draws on open and stolen data abroad. The defenders' own audience data was largely for sale until 2024. \[11\] \[21\]

**Argued.** Retraining changes weights and can leave the design underneath intact, and a licensing arrangement that keeps the original developer at the table keeps a channel open that a clean sale would have closed. Verifying either the platform's objective function or the practitioner's target model requires an audit the public cannot see, run by parties the public did not choose. The side with the richer data runs the sharper analysis on both halves of this case, and the defenders sold theirs into a market that asked no security question when it changed hands.

#### Gap 5. Detection Runs on Volunteers

*Application Architecture*

**Documented.** Meta, OpenAI and outside researchers published the takedowns of the Party's covert networks that this chapter cites. The Global Engagement Center, the one federal office built to expose foreign propaganda, lapsed in December 2024 after critics charged it with funding censorship of Americans. No statutory successor replaced it. \[6\] \[9\] \[23\]

**Argued.** The defenders' detection routine depends entirely on private firms choosing to look for these networks and choosing to publish what they find, with no obligation running either way. The government's own office foundered because critics saw it policing Americans' speech, the exact risk any public routine in this space must avoid, and the fear of repeating that mistake has so far kept a successor from being built at all. A volunteer system finds what a platform is willing to find.

#### Gap 6. An Operation Without an Ethics Gate

*Application Architecture*

**Documented.** The American campaign in the Philippines targeted trust in a vaccine during a pandemic, in a partner nation, through fake accounts built to look Filipino. The Defense Department did not deny the Reuters report and said it runs operations to counter adversary influence. \[14\]

**Argued.** A routine that can target public health burns credibility every later operation needs, at home and abroad. The defenders' strongest weapon against the Party's operations is the simple fact that their news is true and the Party's fabrications are not. An operation like the Philippines campaign spends that asset for a tactical gain, and the account does not reset when the campaign ends. No standing rule currently prevents a future operation from making the same trade.

#### Gap 7. The Truthful Channel Shrank, and No Identity Layer Replaced It

*Technology Architecture*

**Documented.** The 2025 order cut the broadcasters that reach the Party's own audience, and courts have heard the challenges since, with the outcome still unresolved as of this edition. Douyin's hard limits rest on real-name registration that TikTok's foreign defaults do not share and, in a free society, should not. \[1\] \[15\]

**Argued.** The Party runs operations into the defenders' open channels while closing its own, and the defenders' one channel into the Party's closed audience runs on truthful news. Silencing it, whatever its management faults, hands the Party that audience uncontested rather than contested. A free society rightly refuses the Party's identity layer as the price of protecting minors, which means any protection it builds has to work without a registry of citizens behind it, through defaults and device-level signals rather than through the state knowing who is fourteen.

### 7. Target Architecture

The target strengthens both the platform and the audience, labels rather than removes, and polices no citizen's lawful speech.

**Gap to target**

| Gap | Target element | Owner | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. No owner of the objective function | Disclosure and independent audit of recommender objectives above a size threshold | Congress and the Federal Trade Commission | Litigation under the First Amendment, trade-secret disputes |
| 2. The home audience has no owner | Public attribution of foreign operations, with labels and no removal of lawful speech | An office under statutory limits | Political suspicion, attribution errors |
| 3. The Party moves faster | A standing reciprocity rule for platforms, and pre-approved themes for operations abroad | Congress, Commerce, Defense and State | Retaliation, and risk at the edge of delegation |
| 4. The data advantage | Independent audits of training and objectives for foreign-licensed platforms, and limits on data sales | Commerce and Congress | Cost, access to proprietary code, lost broker revenue |
| 5. Detection runs on volunteers | An intelligence office restricted to attribution, with published rules | Executive branch, under statute | Political suspicion, errors of attribution |
| 6. No ethics gate | A standing rule that no operation targets public health or safety information | Defense leadership and Congress | Fewer options in a crisis |
| 7. The channel shrank, no identity layer | Statutory independence and audit for truthful broadcasting, and privacy-preserving age signals set at the device | Congress, device makers and app stores | Budget, management reform, uneven adoption |

**Labels, not removals.** The *COVID-19* case shows in detail what speech policing costs a free society, in trust as much as in law. Every element of this target discloses, audits and labels, and none of them gives any office the power to take a lawful post down.

**Truth is the defenders' advantage.** The Party's operations run on fabrication from end to end. Synthetic personas, fabricated events and manufactured consensus do the work no honest account could do. The defenders' strongest instrument abroad is accurate news the Party structurally cannot supply, because supplying it would require the Party to say things about itself it will not say. The defenders' strongest instrument at home is an audience that recognizes manipulation before it works rather than after, which is exactly what prebunking research shows can be built.

**The ground is the defenders' half.** A government can regulate the platform or force its sale, as the TikTok case shows it can. Only a society can tend the ground underneath. Rigorous schools do that work. So does a defensible baseline of shared fact, and so do institutions of knowledge that have to earn trust back one accurate report at a time. No government can do it for them.

**What the target would not do.** It would not stop the Party's networks, match their volume, or build a domestic operation against the defenders' own citizens under any name. That claim remains an argument, offered on the same terms as every other argument in this chapter.

### What Accommodate Proves

It proves the objective function is governance, on both halves of the case. The Party governs its population's feed through a registry and a regulator that doubles as a Party office, and it governs its target audiences abroad through the same management plane. The defenders let revenue govern one half and leave the other with no owner at all.

It proves Agency is the target throughout. Not a belief, not a platform, but a population's capacity to decide what is true and choose what to do about it.

It proves the discipline cuts both ways. The Philippines campaign shows the defenders can misuse the same craft the Party runs at scale, which is why the ethics gate matters as much as the technique.

It proves the brittleness of a defense aimed at one instrument. Five and a half years removed one company, with the Party's approval, and left the class of weapon standing.

It proves that treating the platform and the practitioner as separate problems was itself part of the defect. The first edition ran these as two chapters, and a reader could reasonably conclude that TikTok's algorithm and a covert influence network were different kinds of threat requiring different kinds of answer. They are the same threat at two layers of one stack, aimed at the same target, and an office that owns one without the other owns half a problem. The merge that produced this chapter is not only an editorial choice. It states the argument in miniature.

As in every case, the Chinese people are not the authors. They live under the same machine, set to a different dial. A society with a high baseline of shared fact and functioning trust is close to immune to this weapon, because the seed dies in healthy soil. The deepest counter is not a policy toward the Party, but a society that has put its own mind back in order.

### Field Card

**What happened.** A Chinese company runs two versions of one recommendation engine, hardening children's attention at home while leaving it soft abroad, and the same state runs covert networks and psychological operations through the platforms this asymmetry created. Congress spent five and a half years forcing one divestiture, which the Party had to approve, while its own operations moved in weeks. No American office owns either the objective function behind the algorithm or the resilience of the audience these operations target.

**The gap.** Governance has no owner in two places at once, the objective function and the home audience's resilience.

**The fix.** Disclosure and independent audit of recommender objectives, paired with public attribution of foreign operations that labels without removing lawful speech, would give both halves of this case an owner.

**Where you could work on it.** Platform accountability and algorithm audit policy, psychological operations or open-source analysis inside government, or media literacy and prebunking research aimed at making a population a harder target.
