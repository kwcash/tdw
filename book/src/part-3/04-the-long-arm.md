## The Long Arm

*Transnational Repression and the Family at Home*

**DOMAINS** Information · Psychological · Legal · **Association (spine)** · Cyber · Data and Surveillance

**DIMENSIONS** Time · Entropy · Topology · **Asymmetry (signature)** · Agency · Resonance · Phase · Gradient · Criticality · **Coherence (master)**

**STRATAGEMS** Spine S13 · S15 · S18 · S22 · S35

**GAPS** Six, one at each layer. Signature gap at the Data layer, where the defenders see the person and the Party sees the family.

In 2018 a man knocked on the door of a house in New Jersey, circled it to look inside and taped a note to the door. The house belonged to Xu Jin, a former official from Wuhan whom the Party wanted back to face bribery allegations he denies. The note said that if Xu went home and served ten years, his "wife and children will be all right." \[1\]

The note was one step in a campaign. The Party had targeted Xu and his wife under its Fox Hunt and Sky Net programs since 2012. From 2016 to 2019 its agents hired a retired New York police sergeant as a private investigator, surveilled the family and pressed them to return. A federal jury convicted three men in June 2023. They received sentences of 16, 18 and 24 months. The retired sergeant told the court he had never thought he was working for the Party. \[1\]

In early 2022 two men opened a branch of the Ministry of Public Security on a floor of a building in Manhattan's Chinatown. It renewed Chinese driver's licenses. It also identified pro-democracy activists living in the United States. The FBI arrested both men in April 2023, and one pleaded guilty in December 2024 to conspiring to act as an agent of the People's Republic. \[2\]

In September 2020 prosecutors charged Baimadajie Angwang, a Tibetan-born New York police officer and Marine veteran, with reporting on New York's Tibetans to the Chinese consulate. He spent about six months in detention. In January 2023 the government dropped every charge in the interests of justice and never explained why. His lawyer said the consulate contacts were attempts to get visas to visit his family. \[3\]

The record documents all three cases. The same lever runs through them, a person abroad tied to a family within the Party's reach. Reading them as one architecture is the framework's argument.

### The Terms of This Case

Transnational repression is a state reaching across borders to watch, threaten, coerce or return people it considers its own. Freedom House's decade of data, from 2014 to 2024, records 1,219 direct physical incidents by 48 governments. The Party's government committed 272 of them, more than any other. Freedom House calls the Party's effort the most sophisticated, global and comprehensive campaign of its kind. \[4\]

The actor is the Party. The targets are overwhelmingly Chinese. They include dissidents and former officials, Uyghurs and Tibetans. Hong Kongers, Falun Gong practitioners and students face the same pressure. Many are citizens of the country where the pressure finds them.

Mao named the instrument that organizes this reach in 1939. "The united front, armed struggle and Party building are the Chinese Communist Party's three magic weapons," he wrote. The united front came first on his list. \[8\] The United Front Work Department that answers to that principle today does not run the campaigns this case documents. It cultivates the hometown associations, professional groups and community organizations the campaigns hide inside, work Mao named as decisive eighty years before a Manhattan police station proved it.

This case carries the book's hardest test of its guardrail. The *Military-Civil Fusion* case holds that the answer is to cut the lever and shield the people. Here the lever is the family at home, and the people are the diaspora the Party pressures. A defense that treats the diaspora with suspicion does the Party's work twice. It isolates the target and it confirms the Party's claim that overseas Chinese have no home but the People's Republic.

The Angwang case belongs in this case for that reason. The record does not say what the new information was. It does show a man who carried the lever and bore the costs of both governments' attention.

### 1. Architecture Vision

**Documented.** The Party runs Fox Hunt and Sky Net as programs to return people it accuses of corruption. Its Ministry of Public Security operates abroad without host-country consent, as the Manhattan case shows. Hong Kong's police have posted bounties of HK$1 million on activists living overseas. \[1\] \[2\] \[5\]

**Argued.** The aim is a population with no exit. The Party means that leaving the mainland does not end its reach, so that no critic abroad speaks freely and no official abroad feels safe with what he took.

### 2. The Party's Baseline

#### Business Architecture. The Capabilities It Fielded

The Association domain carried the operation, because the method works through the ties between a person abroad and people at home.

**Information.** On the same day as the Manhattan arrests in April 2023, the Justice Department charged thirty-four officers of the Ministry of Public Security's 912 Special Project Working Group. Prosecutors said they ran thousands of fake online personas to harass dissidents abroad. \[2\]

**Psychological.** The note on Xu's door needed no violence. It named his wife and children.

**Legal.** The Party frames each return as law enforcement against fugitives. It denies running police stations abroad and calls them service centers.

**Association.** Hometown and community associations give the operation a local face. The Manhattan station occupied a floor of a community building.

**Cyber.** Harassment runs through fake accounts, doxxing and coordinated reporting that gets targets' accounts suspended.

**Data and Surveillance.** The dossier joins the person abroad to relatives at home.

#### Data Architecture. What It Knew

The Party's decisive data is a join. On one side sits the person abroad, located through open records, community contacts and private investigators. On the other sit the relatives at home. The Party can visit or detain them, cost them their jobs or deny them travel. The *Digital Twin* case describes the dossiers that hold both.

**Documented.** Amnesty International and other groups have recorded Uyghurs abroad whose relatives in Xinjiang faced detention or pressure after the Uyghurs spoke out. Fox Hunt prosecutors described relatives in China pressed to plead with the target. \[6\]

#### Application Architecture. The Routines It Ran

The return campaign reads as a business process. It locates the target, then maps the family and applies pressure at home. It delivers an ultimatum abroad and closes with a return or a silence.

Criminal psychology has another name for the same shape. Behavioral analysts who study predatory control describe selection, isolation and extraction as stages a coercive relationship follows regardless of its domain. \[7\] The Party's campaign selects a target, isolates him by mapping and pressuring the family that once felt like support, and extracts a return or a silence. The pattern names a method used by a small number of officials. It says nothing about the millions of people abroad who experience none of it.

**Stratagem 15, Lure the Tiger Down from the Mountain**, draws the target out of the safety of a foreign jurisdiction.

**Stratagem 22, Shut the Door to Catch the Thief**, closes the exits through bounties and pressure on the family.

**Stratagem 18, Capture the Ringleader to Disband the Bandits**, aims at the organizers of diaspora opposition.

**Stratagem 13, Stomp the Grass to Scare the Snake**, lets one visible case frighten every other target.

**Stratagem 35, Chain the Stratagems**, links the location to the family and the family to the ultimatum. The record documents each step in the Fox Hunt trial. The chain across cases is the framework's reading.

#### A Target Audience Note

The pressure campaign against Xu Jin fits this form, and his case serves as the example.

**Documented.** The Party targeted Xu and his wife under Fox Hunt and Sky Net from 2012, hired a private investigator to surveil them from 2016 to 2019, and left a note at their door naming his wife and children by implication. \[1\]

**Argued.** A target audience analysis would read the audience as former officials and dissidents with family still on the mainland, not the diaspora broadly. Their vulnerability runs through exactly the tie that once anchored them to home, a family the Party can visit, detain or deny travel. The line of persuasion holds that the family's safety depends on the target's choice, stated plainly enough that no explicit threat is legally provable. Susceptibility runs highest where the target still believes the relationship with the mainland runs two ways, family loyalty as well as state reach. Accessibility runs through the family itself, which needs no recruitment and no payment, only its own existence within the Party's borders.

#### Technology Architecture. The Platform It Ran On

The overseas stations work as edge nodes of the management plane described in *Military-Civil Fusion*. Their front end offered ordinary services such as license renewals. Behind the front end sat the targeting.

#### Principles and Governance. How It Held Together

**Asymmetry.** The Party reaches into open societies, and no open society can reach the family in Xinjiang or Fujian.

**Coherence.** The public security ministry, the discipline commission, united-front networks and local police at home act on the same target. The record documents the programs and the cases. One design across them is the framework's argument.

### 3. The Defenders' Baseline

No single enterprise protects the targeted person. The FBI investigates crimes, local police take the first call, the State Department handles diplomats and schools handle students.

#### Business Architecture. Who Owned What

| Capability | Defender owner | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| Investigating agents of foreign governments | FBI and the Justice Department | Must prove the agent acted at a foreign government's direction |
| First response to threats | Local police | Rarely trained to recognize a foreign campaign |
| Diplomats who direct operations | State Department | Expulsion is political and rare |
| Students and scholars on campus | Universities | No mandate for foreign threats |
| Records that reveal an address | States and counties | Public by default |
| The family at home | No owner | Beyond reach |

#### Data Architecture. What the Defenders Knew

The defenders see the person in front of them. They seldom see the relative in a mainland city whose job or freedom the Party is using. Xu and his family tried to keep their address out of public records, as the Justice Department later noted. Records built for openness became the location layer the campaign needed. \[1\]

#### Application Architecture. The Routines the Defenders Ran

The defenders' main routine is prosecution under the law against acting as an unregistered agent of a foreign government, often paired with stalking charges. The Fox Hunt verdict and the Manhattan plea show the routine working. The Angwang dismissal shows its risk. A charge that rests on proving direction by a foreign government can reach the wrong person, and a wrong charge lands on the community the Party already pressures. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\]

The timeline runs long. The Party targeted Xu from 2012. The verdict came in 2023 and the last sentence in April 2025.

#### Technology Architecture. The Platforms the Defenders Used

The defenders' platforms carried the harassment. The 912 group's fake personas ran on open American platforms. Public records carried the addresses.

#### Principles and Governance. What Held the Defenders Together

The defenders keep their principles. Their courts demand proof, their prosecutors dropped a case when the evidence changed, and their communities stay open to people from anywhere. The Angwang case shows those principles correcting an error. It also shows the cost of the error to a man who had done his government's work in Afghanistan.

### 4. Gap Analysis

**The principles audit**

| Principle | The Party's design | The defenders' design |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | Pursues a target for years | Resolves a case years after the pressure began |
| **Entropy** | One apparatus acts on target and family | The target navigates several offices alone |
| **Topology** | A hub that joins home and abroad | Offices that each see one piece |
| **Asymmetry** | Reaches abroad and holds the family at home | Cannot reach the family |
| **Agency** | Captures the target's choices through fear | Leaves the target to decide alone whether to report |
| **Resonance** | One visible case frightens every other target | Each case handled as a single crime |
| **Phase** | Applies pressure at home and abroad together | Answers after the ultimatum arrives |
| **Gradient** | Fear tilts the target toward silence | Fear of suspicion tilts the community away from reporting |
| **Criticality** | Aims at organizers and at people who hold money or secrets | Protects no one in advance |
| **Coherence** | Governed by one authority | No owner of the targeted person |

The cells summarize documented acts. The audit itself is the framework's reading.

#### Gap 1. No Owner of the Targeted Person

*Architecture Governance*

**Documented.** The FBI, local police, the State Department and universities each hold a piece of the response. \[1\] \[2\]

**Argued.** The target meets the Party's apparatus as one force and the defenders' response as a set of doors. A frightened person who does not know which door to try often tries none.

#### Gap 2. The Pressure Runs for Years

*Architecture Principles, Time*

**Documented.** The Party targeted Xu from 2012. His pressure campaign ran from 2016 to 2019, and the court sentenced the last defendant in April 2025. \[1\]

**Argued.** The defenders' routine closes a case. The Party's campaign persists across cases and across years, and the target lives inside it the whole time.

#### Gap 3. The Defenders See the Person, the Party Sees the Family

*Data Architecture. The signature gap*

**Documented.** Uyghurs abroad report relatives detained or pressed after they spoke out. Fox Hunt pressure ran through family members. Angwang's lawyer said his consulate contacts sought visas to visit family. \[3\] \[6\]

**Argued.** The lever is a record the defenders cannot see and a person they cannot protect. The Party holds both ends of the join. The defenders hold one, and they sometimes read the target's contact with the Party as a sign of loyalty to it.

#### Gap 4. The Community Is Venue and Victim

*Business Architecture*

**Documented.** The Manhattan station operated from a community building. The Angwang case, and the China Initiative's drift recorded in *Technology IP*, left parts of the Chinese American community wary of federal attention. \[2\] \[3\]

**Argued.** The Party hides its edge nodes inside the community it pressures. A defense that treats the community as suspect loses the reports it most needs. No public owner builds the trust that would bring those reports in.

#### Gap 5. The Law Names the Agent, Not the Act

*Application Architecture*

**Documented.** Prosecutors charge acting as an unregistered foreign agent, stalking and obstruction. The Angwang charges collapsed. \[1\] \[2\] \[3\]

**Argued.** A routine built on proving who directs a person must investigate the person's ties to a foreign state. That puts the diaspora's family ties at the center of every case. A law aimed at the act of coercion on a foreign government's behalf would center the harm instead of the ancestry of the harmed.

#### Gap 6. The Front End Looked Like a Service Desk

*Technology Architecture*

**Documented.** The Manhattan station renewed licenses. The 912 group used fake personas on open platforms. \[2\]

**Argued.** The Party's edge nodes present ordinary interfaces to ordinary people. The targeting runs behind the interface, on platforms and records the defenders built to be open.

### 5. Target Architecture

Every element serves the guardrail. It cuts the lever and shields the people, and it never sorts anyone by ancestry.

**Gap to target**

| Gap | Target element | Owner | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. No owner of the targeted person | One protective owner with a single point of contact in community languages | The FBI, with local police and victim services | Staff, and trust built over years |
| 2. The pressure runs for years | Standing protection for known targets that outlasts any single case | The same owner | Long-term cost |
| 3. The defenders see the person | Treat contact under family pressure as evidence of coercion, not of loyalty, and document it that way | Investigators and prosecutors | Harder cases against real agents |
| 4. The community is venue and victim | Funded trusted intermediaries, and no screening by ancestry or origin | Community groups, with federal support | Money, and patience |
| 5. The law names the agent | A statute naming transnational repression as a crime of coercion on a foreign government's behalf, with published safeguards against misuse | Congress | Drafting difficulty, and civil-liberties review |
| 6. The front end looked like a service desk | Address confidentiality for targets, action against state-run fake personas and closure of undeclared foreign police offices | States, platforms and the State Department | Administrative cost, and diplomatic friction |

**Expel the directors.** A documented campaign traced to named officials should bring a standing, pre-agreed response against those officials and the units they serve, as the *Hong Kong* case argues for pre-authorized packages.

**Address confidentiality works already.** Many states run confidential-address programs for victims of domestic violence. Extending them to targets of foreign coercion costs little and shuts the location layer the campaign used against Xu.

**What the target would not do.** It would not free the family at home. Nothing a free society does in New Jersey reaches a relative in Wuhan. It would make the target easier to protect and harder to find, and it would make the community a partner rather than a suspect. That claim remains an argument.

### What the Long Arm Proves

It proves Asymmetry at its most personal. The Party reaches into open societies through the family it holds at home, and no open society can reach back.

It proves the lifecycle's Consume stage aimed at individuals. The Party locates the target and pressures the family. It controls the target's choices and consumes his freedom, whether he returns or falls silent.

It proves the guardrail is the strategy. A defense that treats the targeted community as suspect hands the Party both the victim and the argument. A defense that shields the community and cuts the lever takes both away.

The Angwang case belongs at the center of the lesson. The same lever that the Party pulled was the one his own government read as guilt, and the courts corrected the error only after it had cost him his liberty and his career. Cut the lever. Shield the people.

### Field Card

**What happened.** A former Wuhan official living in New Jersey found a note on his door naming his wife and children, the latest step in a campaign the Party had run against him since 2012 using hired investigators and two named repatriation programs. A federal jury convicted three men in 2023, and courts later dismissed unrelated charges against a New York police officer whose own consulate contacts were never proven to be anything but an attempt to see his family. Both cases turned on the same fact, a family on the mainland the target could not protect and the defenders could not reach.

**The gap.** No one on the defenders' side can see the family the Party is pressuring, the signature gap at the Data layer, and no single office owns the targeted person either.

**The fix.** A single protective point of contact for targets, paired with treating contact made under family pressure as evidence of coercion rather than loyalty, would close both gaps without policing the diaspora.

**Where you could work on it.** Federal law enforcement focused on transnational repression, victim services for diaspora communities, or legislative work drafting a statute that names coercion on a foreign government's behalf as the crime rather than the target's foreign ties.
