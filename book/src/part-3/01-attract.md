## Attract

*The Borrowed Knife*

**DOMAINS** Economic · **Financial (spine)** · Information · Legal · Association · Data and Surveillance

**DIMENSIONS** Time · Entropy · Topology · Asymmetry · Agency · Resonance · Phase · **Gradient (signature)** · Criticality · Coherence

**STRATAGEMS** Spine S1 · S3 · S9 · S11 · S35

**GAPS** Six, one at each layer. Signature gap at the Principles layer, where every gate earned a fee for opening and none for staying shut.

In 58 BC a delegation of Aedui chiefs came to Julius Caesar in tears and begged for help. Diviciacus, their leader, said the choice was stark. Unless Caesar's army intervened, the Gauls would have to abandon their homeland the way the Helvetii already had. \[22\] Caesar intervened. He broke Ariovistus and drove the Germans back across the Rhine. He did not then leave. Caesar spent the next seven years conquering the rest of Gaul, the Aedui included, and by 52 BC the same tribe that had begged him for protection stood in revolt against him at Alesia and lost.

The Aedui invited a protector because they needed one, and the protector never left. That arc, invitation to conquest, is the shape of the five chapters ahead. Part Three follows one campaign through five stages. Attract. Accommodate. Control. Extract. Consume. This chapter is the first stage, and the knife the Party borrowed was never stolen. Collaborators handed it over, one fee at a time.

On September 19, 2014, a retail investor anywhere in America could buy a hundred shares of Alibaba. The offering raised $21.8 billion, the largest initial public offering in history to that point. Six of Wall Street's largest banks stood on the cover. \[1\]

He did not buy a hundred shares of Alibaba. He bought shares in a Cayman Islands holding company that owned no part of Alibaba's Chinese operating business and never had. The holding company owned a stack of contracts. Service agreements, powers of attorney and equity pledges routed the operating company's economics offshore without transferring its ownership. The accounting rules let the shell consolidate the operating company, so the statements read as though the investor owned a piece of it. He owned a claim on a claim.

The prospectus disclosed the structure deep in its pages. Seven years later the chairman of the Securities and Exchange Commission put the buried fact on the record. On July 30, 2021, Gary Gensler stated that neither the shell nor its shareholders own equity in the operating business. He added his concern that ordinary investors did not grasp the difference. \[2\]

By March 2025 the U.S.-China Economic and Security Review Commission counted 286 Chinese companies on the three largest American exchanges, worth $1.1 trillion together. It counted 159 of them running on this contractual shell, the variable interest entity or VIE. Those issuers carried about $1 trillion, ninety-one percent of all the Chinese market value listed in the United States. \[1\]

Every gate on the way to that trillion was lawful, and every gate earned a fee for opening. The underwriters took a spread on each offering. The auditors took their fees. The exchanges took listing fees and the index providers took licensing fees. The fund managers took a fee on every dollar the index sent them. No one on that road earned a dollar for saying no.

The record documents each gate and each fee. Reading them as one architecture that the Party used without ever recruiting it is the framework's argument.

### The Terms of This Case

The case turns on one discipline. The conduct in this record falls into three categories, and blurring them destroys the case.

*Category one is wrongdoing that courts and regulators have punished.* The system worked there. Luckin Coffee paid a $180 million penalty for fabricated sales. Once the audit board gained access to the mainland, it fined the local affiliates of major audit firms. \[6\] \[7\]

*Category two is lawful conduct, however harmful.* The underwriters and auditors did lawful work on disclosed and registered offerings. So did the exchanges, the index providers, the asset managers and the regulators who registered and supervised the deals. None of them touched the mainland's operating companies. This case calls them the remote collaborators, in the plain sense of the word. They worked on the same deals the Party needed done. The record shows none of them working for the Party and none of them enlisted. The system did not need traitors. It needed incentives, and it had them.

*Category three is open questions.* The record raises questions that only government investigators can answer, and the case answers none of them. The questions appear with the target architecture, framed as grounds for investigation and never as findings.

The actor throughout is the Party. The founders of these companies are entrepreneurs, and their employees and customers are ordinary people on the mainland. The Party wrote the wall that keeps foreign owners out, and it decides which companies may list abroad. It reached into DiDi and the private tutoring sector in 2021 and cost mainland workers and families as well as American holders. \[14\]

### 1. Architecture Vision

**Documented.** The Party bars foreign ownership of companies in its internet and data sectors, and in media, education and finance. Its securities regulator has required every company that lists abroad to file for approval since March 31, 2023. VIE structures fall under the rule. The National Intelligence Law of 2017 obliges every organization on the mainland to support state intelligence work. \[3\] \[13\]

**Argued.** The design lets the Party take foreign capital while conceding no foreign control. The money crosses the wall into the operating companies. The ownership and the data stay behind it under the Party's law, and so do the licenses and the intellectual property. The capital built the technology sector that *Technology IP* shows drawing in American knowledge by other channels.

### 2. The Party's Baseline

#### Business Architecture. The Capabilities It Fielded

The Financial domain carried the operation, because the capital market itself was the knife.

**Economic.** The capital went to the sectors the Party's industrial plans name as the high ground. By the Commission's classification, more than half the VIE cohort's value sits in companies tagged as technology. E-commerce, fintech, electric vehicles and logistics fill most of the rest. \[1\]

**Financial.** An American listing created a liquid dollar currency in which these companies could pay their people. Alibaba's share-based compensation rose from RMB 1,254 million in fiscal 2012 to RMB 16,082 million in fiscal 2016, across the listing. JD.com's ran from RMB 4,250 million in its 2014 listing year to RMB 9,134 million at the 2021 peak, then fell to RMB 2,999 million in 2024. The pay climbed with American valuations and fell when American appetite withdrew. American investors supplied the standing bid that made the equity worth granting. \[11\]

**Information.** The structure hid in its own disclosures. The prospectus front sections called the Chinese business "our" business, and the qualifications sat hundreds of pages deep.

**Legal.** The Party's law built both walls. One wall bars foreign ownership of the operating companies. The other, state-secrecy law, kept the American audit board out of the mainland's audit firms for most of two decades. \[4\]

**Association.** The Party recruited no one on the American side. The fee structure of the capital market aligned thousands of separate professionals with the listing of each deal.

**Data and Surveillance.** The operating companies hold the data, and the data sits under the Party's law. When the Party's cyberspace regulator opened its review of DiDi in July 2021, it did so on data-security grounds. \[14\]

#### Data Architecture. What It Knew

Here the record documents the Party's data layer rather than leaving it to inference. The Party holds the operating companies' books, their user data and their audit papers, and its secrecy law decides who may read them. For most of two decades the American audit board could not.

The Party also knew what the audit papers of its state-owned enterprises contained. When the audit confrontation came to a head, five state-owned giants announced their exit from American exchanges on August 12, 2022. The Party signed the audit protocol two weeks later. \[4\] \[16\]

#### Application Architecture. The Routines It Ran

**Stratagem 3, Kill with a Borrowed Knife**, is the master routine of the case. The Party funds its champions with the rival's capital. It bars the foreign ownership that would allow a direct listing, then approves the VIE workaround that allows a listing anyway. The record documents the design of the precondition. Whether the Party foresaw the full scale of the transfer is unknowable.

**Stratagem 1, Deceive the Heavens to Cross the Sea**, is the VIE itself. The structure crossed the sea every trading day in plain sight, normalized by repetition and hidden in the footnotes.

Stratagem 11 is **Sacrifice the Plum Tree to Save the Peach.** The state-owned enterprises whose audit papers held real secrets left American reach. The consumer internet and the technology champions stayed and kept taking American capital. The record documents the timing. Reading the exit as a deliberate sacrifice is the framework's argument.

**Stratagem 9, Watch the Fire from Across the River**, describes the rupture of 2021. The Party's regulator opened its review of DiDi two days after the company's $4.4 billion New York offering, and app stores removed DiDi's app two days after that. A decree against private tutoring gutted the education listings the same month. The losses fell on foreign holders, and the control stayed with the Party. \[14\]

**Stratagem 35, Chain the Stratagems**, links the rest. The wall creates the need and the shell answers it. The collaborators process it, and the secrecy law keeps the books closed. The record shows each link. The chain is the framework's reading.

#### A Target Audience Note

The American retail and default-fund investor fits this form, and the prospectus itself is the channel.

**Documented.** VIE prospectuses describe the Chinese operating business as "our" business in their opening pages, with the contractual qualification appearing hundreds of pages deep. Index inclusion put these listings into target-date funds and default 401(k) allocations without any individual purchase decision. \[9\]

**Argued.** A target audience analysis would read the audience as two groups rather than one. The first is the investor who reads a prospectus and stops before the qualification. The second, larger group never opens a prospectus at all, and holds the exposure only because a benchmark included it. Vulnerability in the first group runs through complexity and length. A filing that runs hundreds of pages rewards a reader who stops early. Vulnerability in the second group is structural rather than psychological. No decision of theirs is available to target, because no decision was made. Susceptibility runs highest among savers in a default plan who never chose an allocation and would not recognize the company name if they saw it. Accessibility runs through the fund's own quarterly statement, if the saver reads that far, and through nothing at all if he does not. The channel that reaches this audience most effectively is the one that requires no persuasion, because the target never has to agree to anything.

#### Technology Architecture. The Platform It Ran On

The operating company is the platform, and the Party administers it. The operating company holds the licenses without which the business cannot run. The Party's regulators grant those licenses, approve each overseas listing and can review any company on data grounds. The shareholder in New York holds a contract with a shell. The Party holds administrator rights over everything the contract describes.

#### Second-Order Effects. What the Capital Bought

The money kept working after the listing.

**Documented. Talent.** The share-based compensation in the companies' own filings rose with American valuations and fell when American buyers left. American investors underwrote the currency these companies used to hire engineers and managers. \[11\]

**Documented. Control.** The Party took special management shares, often about one percent, in the onshore units of the largest platforms. A fund set up by its cyberspace regulator took one percent of an Alibaba unit in January 2023 and one percent of a Tencent unit in October 2023. The Tencent stake cost 600,000 yuan, about $82,000. The ByteDance stake of April 2021 came with the right to name one of three directors of a key onshore company. The Party's company law requires every company to host Party organizations and to support their work. \[20\] \[21\]

**Documented. Military capital.** The index channel reached beyond the VIE companies. The bipartisan House committee found that American index and fund money put more than $5.3 billion into the Party's military companies in 2023 alone. \[18\]

**Argued. The flywheel.** The capital pays the engineers, and the engineers produce research, patents and products. The products raise the valuation that attracts the next round of capital. Each turn of the wheel builds the technology base that *Technology IP* shows the Party filling by patent, recruitment and intrusion as well.

**Argued. Gift money.** A VIE shareholder holds a contractual claim on profits, so in law the money is an investment. In control it behaves like a gift. The giver keeps no vote over the operating company, no seat on its board and no recourse the Party's courts have shown they will enforce. A one-percent state fund holds a board seat that a trillion dollars of American capital never bought.

**Argued. The tax channel.** The operating companies pay taxes into the state budget, and the state budget funds the People's Liberation Army. Every profitable VIE company contributes to that budget, as every profitable company on the mainland does.

**Not in the record.** This edition found no document that traces VIE proceeds into a Party slush fund, into bribery or into a specific theft of technology. A source that shows any of those enters the case. Until one does, the case claims none of them.

#### Principles and Governance. How It Held Together

**Asymmetry.** The American investor supplies ownership capital and receives a contract. The Party supplies nothing and keeps control.

**Phase.** The Party moved within days when it chose to. Its regulator reached DiDi two days after the offering, and its state-owned enterprises left two weeks before the audit protocol.

**Coherence.** One authority writes the ownership wall, approves each listing, controls the audit papers and can review any listed company. The record documents each of those powers. Their coordination is the framework's argument.

### 3. The Defenders' Baseline

No single enterprise defended the American saver. Public regulators held his defense with a mandate of disclosure. Security agencies held lists that do not meet, and private collaborators earned their pay from the deal.

#### Business Architecture. Who Owned What

**The remote collaborators**

| Gate | Collaborator | Paid for | Paid to refuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-listing capital | Venture firms | Fees and a share of the gains | No |
| Listing | Underwriting banks | A spread on each offering | No |
| Audit | Mainland and Hong Kong affiliates of the major audit firms | Audit fees | No |
| Exchange | NYSE and Nasdaq | Listing and trading fees | No |
| Index | MSCI and FTSE Russell | Licensing fees on each benchmark | No |
| Allocation | Asset managers and pension trustees | Fees on assets under management | No |
| Registration | Securities and Exchange Commission | A mandate to require disclosure, not to judge merit | Not its mandate |
| Audit oversight | Public Company Accounting Oversight Board | A mandate to inspect every listed issuer's auditor | Blocked for most of two decades |
| Retirement rules | Department of Labor | A mandate to police fiduciary duty | No security test until the 2025 memorandum asked for one |

Every private gate on the road earned money when a deal went through. None earned money when a deal stopped. The public gates held mandates for disclosure, inspection and prudence, and none held a mandate for security.

**The congressional finding.** The bipartisan House Select Committee on the CCP reported in April 2024 that index providers and asset managers facilitated more than $6.5 billion of investment in 63 red-flagged mainland companies in 2023. It found that MSCI indexes channeled $3.7 billion to those companies and that BlackRock invested at least $1.9 billion. More than $5.3 billion went to the Party's military companies, including makers of fighter jets and nuclear weapons for the PLA. In February 2024 the same committee reported that five American venture firms invested at least $3 billion in mainland AI and semiconductor companies. Many of those companies aid the Party's military or its surveillance state. \[18\] \[19\]

**The security owners.** The Department of Defense keeps a list of Chinese military companies under Section 1260H of the 2021 defense act. Treasury keeps a separate list, under an executive order of June 2021, that bars Americans from buying the listed securities. The two lists use different legal tests and name different companies. \[12\]

**The savings owners.** Pension trustees, the Thrift Savings Plan board, the Department of Labor under ERISA and university endowment managers each decide their own exposure. The index providers decide it automatically for anyone holding a default fund.

#### Data Architecture. What the Defenders Knew

The investor's own records described a holding he did not have. The accounting consolidated the operating company onto the shell's statements, so the numbers presented a business the investor did not own.

The government's records did not meet. A November 2020 order barred American investment in companies on the Defense list. Courts then blocked several of those designations. The June 2021 order moved the investment ban to a new Treasury list with its own test, and the Defense list published the same day carried no investment consequence. \[12\]

Hesai shows the result. The lidar maker listed on Nasdaq in February 2023. Defense added it to the 1260H list in January 2024, and a federal court upheld the designation in July 2025. The designation's statutory consequence reaches Defense contracting from 2026. American investors could still buy the stock in 2026. \[12\]

#### Application Architecture. The Routines the Defenders Ran

**The audit clock**

| Date | Owner | Routine | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2002 | Congress | Sarbanes-Oxley creates the audit board with power to inspect every listed issuer's auditor | The mainland blocks inspection for most of two decades |
| December 18, 2020 | Congress | Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act sets a delisting timer | The board finds on December 16, 2021 that it cannot inspect |
| August 12, 2022 | The Party's state-owned enterprises | Five giants announce their exit from American exchanges | They leave before any inspection |
| August 26, 2022 | Audit board and the Party's regulators | Statement of Protocol signed | Inspections begin that autumn |
| December 15, 2022 | Audit board | Reports complete access for the first time | The delisting timer stops |
| May 2023 | Audit board | First inspection findings | Deficiencies in seven of the eight audits examined |
| November 2023 | Audit board | Sanctions totaling $7.9 million | The system works where it can reach |

The delisting routine worked. A published trigger with an automatic consequence produced access the board had sought for two decades. The trigger measured access to audit papers, and the Party granted access. It kept the ownership wall, the licenses and the data.

The fraud routine worked too, within its reach. Luckin Coffee fabricated more than $300 million in sales and paid the SEC $180 million. The Party's market regulator fined the Luckin entities about $9 million. The SEC's own release records that any payment to defrauded shareholders required approval from authorities on the mainland. \[6\] \[7\]

#### Technology Architecture. The Platforms the Defenders Used

The defenders' platforms are the deepest capital markets in the world, and the index is their automatic transmission. Once MSCI and FTSE Russell added Chinese listings to their global and emerging-market benchmarks, every American in a target-date fund or a default 401(k) allocation held these shells by mechanical operation. No person made that decision for the saver. The benchmark made it. \[9\]

#### Principles and Governance. What Held the Defenders Together

The defenders kept their principles. Securities law requires disclosure rather than merit review, so investors may take any risk they understand. Fiduciaries diversify. Markets stay open to foreign issuers. Those principles are worth keeping. They also left the security question with no owner inside the capital market.

The White House memorandum on investment policy of February 21, 2025 committed the administration to review VIE structures, audit adequacy and pension fiduciary standards. It named university endowments among the channels to examine. The memorandum imposed no rule itself. \[15\]

### 4. Gap Analysis

**The principles audit**

| Principle | The Party's design | The defenders' design |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | Took capital for two decades and recalled the secrets in two weeks | Took twenty years to open the audit papers |
| **Entropy** | One authority over the wall, the licenses and the books | Six gates and two security lists, none connected |
| **Topology** | A star around the Party's regulators | A mesh of private collaborators and public agencies |
| **Asymmetry** | Takes a board seat for one percent and hands investors a contract | Supplies ownership capital and receives no seat |
| **Agency** | Decides which companies list and which come home | Leaves each fiduciary and each index to decide alone |
| **Resonance** | Wall, approval, secrecy and data law point one way | Defense designates Hesai and investors still buy it |
| **Phase** | Reached DiDi in two days and pulled the state firms in two weeks | Delisting timer measured in years |
| **Gradient** | Needed only the fee structure the market already had | Every gate paid to open, none paid to refuse |
| **Criticality** | Aimed the capital at technology and data | Pointed default savings at the index automatically |
| **Coherence** | Governed by one authority | No owner of the security question in the capital market |

The cells summarize documented acts. The audit itself is the framework's reading.

#### Gap 1. No Owner of the Security Question

*Architecture Governance*

**Documented.** The SEC's mandate is investor protection through disclosure. Defense and Treasury keep security lists with different tests. The Labor Department governs pension fiduciaries. Congress's defense act for fiscal 2026 ordered a report on harmonizing the sanctions lists. The White House memorandum of 2025 ordered reviews and imposed no rule. \[12\] \[15\]

**Argued.** Each owner behaved correctly inside its own remit. No owner held the question of whether American savings should finance the Party's technology sector. A trillion dollars of listings ran through a capital market that had no seat for the national-security reader.

#### Gap 2. Every Gate Paid to Open

*Architecture Principles, Gradient. The signature gap*

**Documented.** Every private collaborator on the road earned fees when a VIE listing went through. The Commission's table names the underwriting syndicate for each issuer. \[1\] The House committee found in 2024 that index providers and asset managers facilitated more than $6.5 billion of investment in red-flagged mainland companies in a single year. \[18\] The Thrift Savings Plan board moved in 2020 toward a benchmark that included Chinese listings, then reversed and excluded China and Hong Kong outright. \[9\] A 2023 review counted $68 billion committed to China by seventy-two large public pension funds between 2020 and 2023. \[8\]

**Argued.** The Party needed no agent. It needed a slope, and the fee structure of the American capital market supplied one. Every collaborator acted lawfully and profited from the flow. Refusal earned nothing, and the one public fiduciary that refused did so under political pressure rather than by design. The remote collaborators are not the defect. The slope that rewarded every one of them for opening is the defect.

#### Gap 3. The Records Do Not Describe the Holding

*Data Architecture*

**Documented.** Consolidation accounting presents the operating company on the shell's statements. The SEC chairman stated in 2021 that the shareholders own no equity in the operating business. Defense's list and Treasury's list use different tests, and only Treasury's bars investment. Hesai sat on the first and remained open to American buyers. \[2\] \[12\]

**Argued.** The investor's data model says he owns a company, and the law says he holds a contract. The government's data model says a company is military, and the capital market never reads that record. Two schemas describe the same holding, and neither describes it accurately.

#### Gap 4. The Trigger Measured Access, Not Control

*Application Architecture*

**Documented.** The Holding Foreign Companies Accountable Act set a timer that ran on inspection access. The Party's state-owned giants left two weeks before the protocol, and the board gained complete access that December. The first inspections found deficiencies in seven of eight audits. \[4\] \[5\] \[16\]

**Argued.** The routine worked on the variable it measured. The Party conceded access to audit papers and kept the ownership wall, the licenses and the data. A trigger built on control rather than access would have asked for what the Party would not give.

#### Gap 5. The Party Holds Root Over the Operating Company

*Technology Architecture*

**Documented.** DiDi raised $4.4 billion in New York on June 30, 2021. The Party's cyberspace regulator opened a review two days later, and app stores removed DiDi's app on July 4. DiDi completed its New York delisting in June 2022. The Party's securities regulator has approved every overseas listing since March 2023. \[3\] \[14\] A fund set up by the Party's cyberspace regulator took special management shares in onshore units of Alibaba and Tencent in 2023. \[20\]

**Argued.** The shareholder's contract describes a business that runs on the Party's licenses and the Party's data rules. The Party can reach the business in days and leave the shareholder holding the contract. A one-percent state stake buys the Party a board seat. The New York shareholder owns no seat at any price. The Hong Kong case showed controls running on a platform the adversary administers. This case shows capital doing the same.

#### Gap 6. The Index Chose for the Saver

*Business Architecture, Financial*

**Documented.** Index inclusion put Chinese listings into the default funds of American retirement savers. The Thrift Savings Plan chose to exclude them. The 2025 memorandum called for pension fiduciary standards that make adversary companies ineligible and named endowments among the channels to review. \[9\] \[15\]

**Argued.** The capability to allocate national savings with security in view had no owner. Each fiduciary decided alone, and the index provider decided for millions who never decided at all.

### 5. Target Architecture

The target keeps disclosure, open markets and the saver's freedom to take risks he understands. It gives the security question an owner and makes refusal pay.

**Gap to target**

| Gap | Target element | Owner | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. No owner of the security question | The council holds one capital-channel schema across the SEC, Treasury, Defense and Labor, with published thresholds | National Strategic Competition Council, convening only | A statute that outlives an election cycle, and agencies that share their agendas |
| 2. Every gate paid to open | Security screening as a condition of underwriting, index eligibility and default-fund inclusion, with a safe harbor for collaborators who refuse | SEC, Labor and the index providers | Lost fees, narrower benchmarks, and litigation over the screen |
| 3. The records do not describe the holding | One harmonized list of military companies that every capital-market gate reads, and a plain-language ownership label at the point of sale | Treasury and Defense, with the SEC | Designation errors that now reach markets, and more appeals |
| 4. The trigger measured access | Listing conditioned on equity ownership of the operating business, phased in, with the audit timer re-triggered automatically on any obstruction | SEC and the exchanges | Delistings, losses to current holders, and capital that moves to Hong Kong |
| 5. The Party holds root | Standard disclosure of the Party's approval and data powers over each operating company | SEC | Longer prospectuses, and issuers that list elsewhere |
| 6. The index chose for the saver | Exclusion of VIE issuers from the benchmarks that default retirement money tracks | Labor, the Thrift board model, and the index providers | Tracking error, and a smaller opportunity set |

**A published trigger already worked here.** The audit statute set an automatic consequence and produced access the board had sought for twenty years. The target moves the same design from access to ownership.

**The index exclusion does the most for the least disruption.** The Thrift Savings Plan proved a fiduciary can exclude China without forcing a disorderly sale. Extending that choice to default retirement money stops the automatic flow and leaves every investor free to buy the shares on purpose.

**The collaborators need a reason to refuse.** A safe harbor for an underwriter or index provider that declines a listing on security grounds turns refusal from a lost fee into a protected act. The slope then runs both ways.

**Open questions go to investigators.** The record raises questions only government can answer. Did specific issuers misstate the enforceability of their VIE contracts after the SEC tightened disclosure in 2021? Did any audit firm resume obstruction after 2022? Did the sale of these securities to retail and pension investors involve misrepresentation under state law? Did fund prospectuses describe the red-flag risks the House committee said they omitted? Each question belongs with the Justice Department and state attorneys general as grounds for inquiry, and none is a finding.

**The counterweights stand.** Early holders of several of these companies earned large returns, and diversification into the second-largest economy was a defensible mandate. Mainland champions would have found capital in Hong Kong or on the mainland without New York, though at smaller scale and lower valuations. The issuers disclosed and registered every offering. American holdings of mainland securities roughly halved from their 2020 peak, the Thrift Savings Plan excluded China, and outbound investment rules took effect in 2025. \[10\] \[17\] None of this dissolves the finding. It bounds it.

**What the target would not have done.** It would not have stopped mainland companies from raising money elsewhere. It would have stopped American default savings from financing them automatically, and it would have given someone in the capital market the job of asking the security question. That claim remains an argument.

### What the Borrowed Knife Proves

The Borrowed Knife proves Gradient. The Party needed no agent in New York. It needed a market in which every collaborator earned a fee for opening and none for refusing, and the market already existed.

It proves Asymmetry at the level of ownership. The American saver supplied equity and received a contract. The Party supplied nothing and kept the company, its data and its licenses.

It ties the book's money to its technology. The capital that crossed the wall financed the sectors that *Technology IP* shows drawing in American knowledge by patent, recruitment and intrusion.

For a quarter-century the United States ran the deepest capital market in the world with no way to price the difference between owning a company and holding an unenforceable contract on one. It had no seat for anyone to weigh the strategic cost of financing a rival's technology base. The transfer was lawful, systematic and largely still permitted. The knife was never stolen. It lay on the table, and the market paid the people who passed it across.

### Field Card

**What happened.** The Party barred foreign ownership of its technology companies, then approved a contractual workaround that let those companies list in New York anyway. By 2025 that workaround carried about $1 trillion of the $1.1 trillion in Chinese company value listed on American exchanges. Every American gatekeeper along the way earned a fee for letting the listing through and nothing for stopping it. Underwriters and auditors, exchanges and index providers, and fund managers each took their cut.

**The gap.** No one in the American capital market owned the question of whether a listing served national security, the signature gap at the Principles layer.

**The fix.** A published security screen at the point of underwriting, indexing and default-fund inclusion would give every collaborator a paid reason to refuse instead of only a paid reason to proceed.

**Where you could work on it.** Securities and exchange regulation, index and benchmark design, or pension and retirement-plan fiduciary policy.
