## Appendix F. The Consolidated Roadmap

*Every Fix, Sequenced*

Every case chapter in this book closes with a target table, one gap and one fix at a time. Read alone, each fix looks reasonable. Read together, seventy-two of them would overwhelm any legislature, any agency and any budget cycle presented all at once. *The Roadmap* makes exactly that argument. This appendix does the sequencing that chapter only sketched.

TOGAF calls the discipline migration planning, and it sorts change into three transition architectures on the way to a target rather than the target itself. **Now** needs no new statute, because existing authority already covers it. **Next** needs one statute or one rule, not a program of them. **Later** needs years and money before it exists at all, regardless of who is willing.

Each row below carries the chapter it came from, so a reader can return to the full argument behind any single line.

### Reading the Roadmap

Seventy-two elements sort three ways. Thirty-one need no new law at all, only a decision inside an agency that already holds the authority to make it. Thirty-one need one statute or one rule apiece, not a program of them. Ten need years of capacity no statute can shortcut.

The pattern rewards a particular kind of patience. Most of the Now column costs nothing but the will to act, which is why it belongs first, before any legislature convenes. Most of the Next column repeats the same owner, the National Strategic Competition Council, across a dozen different lines. This is *Coherence Without Command*'s argument made visible in a table rather than in prose. Build that one convening body and roughly a third of the Next column becomes easier to pass, because the venue for agreeing on it already exists. The Later column carries the items no administration can rush, and the honest answer to each of them is to start now, because a decade from now is the earliest any of them arrive regardless of when the work begins.

### Sourcing Status: Appendix F

**Primary documents.** Every element in this appendix is drawn directly from the target table of the chapter named in its row. No new documented fact is asserted here that the source chapter does not already carry, and no citation number is repeated, since the source chapter's own Appendix H entry carries it.

**Stated as inference, not finding.** The sorting of each element into Now, Next or Later is this book's own judgment. It weighs what existing authority covers, what a single new rule could reach, and what requires years of capacity regardless of statute. A reader with deeper expertise in a given agency's actual authority may reasonably move some rows between columns. The count of seventy-two, and its rough three-way split, offers a picture of the whole roadmap's shape. No precise inventory that permits no revision is on offer here.
