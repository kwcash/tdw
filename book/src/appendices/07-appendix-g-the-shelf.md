## Appendix G. The Shelf

*Twenty-Five Works, One Line Each*

**Qiao Liang and Wang Xiangsui, *Unrestricted Warfare* (1999).** Names most of this book's twelve domains a generation early, from the perspective of a weaker power. It does not ask what let the stronger power's own architecture go unguarded.

**Robert Spalding, *War Without Rules* (2022).** Reads the adversary's playbook in detail, chapter by chapter. It does not locate the defect inside the defender's own architecture, which is this book's project instead.

**Pat Choate, *A Great Wall of Patents* (2005).** Documents the mining of published patent applications years before anyone else noticed and recommends fixes that were never adopted. It stops at one domain, patents, and does not connect the pattern to the other eleven this book maps.

**Robert Jay Lifton, *Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism* (1961).** Names eight criteria of a totalist system from interviews with people who lived through Chinese thought reform. It describes what the system does to one mind, not what the same design does to twelve domains at once.

**Hannah Arendt, *Eichmann in Jerusalem* (1963).** Names the banality of evil, the ordinary clerk doing ordinary work inside an extraordinary system. It studies one office in one totalitarian bureaucracy, not a whole architecture run across a rival civilization.

**George F. Kennan, "The Inauguration of Organized Political Warfare" (1948).** Proves the United States once named and organized around exactly the whole-of-government instrument this book describes, three years after the war that built it. It never says what became of the instrument, or why the country stopped building it.

**Douglas Pike, *Viet Cong* (1966).** Documents a coherent political organization running every domain of a war a materially weaker side went on to win. It answers how. It does not ask whether the same coherence, run today, would look different with a machine behind it.

**Andrew Mertha, *Brothers in Arms* (2014).** Shows Hanoi's Politburo running a single coordinated struggle against a divided, better-resourced rival bureaucracy. It treats the coordination gap as a historical curiosity rather than a general property any divided system carries.

**Sun Tzu, *The Art of War*, the Giles translation of 1910.** Public domain. States the oldest form of nearly every stratagem this book catalogs, including the case for human sources over documents. It offers no architecture for running the whole catalog across twelve domains at machine speed.

**Niccolò Machiavelli, *The Prince*, the Marriott translation of 1908.** Public domain. States plainly that a ruler keeps faith only while it serves him. It has nothing to say about a guarantee kept by a government answerable to courts and elections instead of a prince.

**The Open Group, *TOGAF Standard*, 10th Edition (2022).** Supplies the whole vocabulary this book borrows. Architecture principles and architecture contracts, migration planning and an architecture board. The standard was built to align one enterprise's own systems, not to read an adversary's campaign against a rival civilization.

**National Institute of Standards and Technology, *Special Publication 800-207*, Zero Trust Architecture (2020).** States the rule that no access is trusted permanently and every request is verified. The publication was written for a computer network, and this book is the argument that the same rule governs a defense built across twelve domains.

**Ivan Arreguín-Toft, *How the Weak Win Wars* (2005).** Built the rigorous modern account of how a materially inferior actor defeats a stronger one, the foundation the asymmetry literature still stands on. It answers how the weak beat the strong, a different question from how a stronger side loses to its own uncoordinated strength.

**Timothy L. Thomas, "Russia's Reflexive Control Theory and the Military" (2004).** Names the Soviet doctrine of feeding an opponent selected information so the opponent's own reasoning delivers the decision the sender wanted. It describes the doctrine in the abstract and leaves its automation, at population scale, to whoever builds the machine.

**Robert D. Hare, *Without Conscience* (1993).** Catalogs the pattern of predatory control, selection and isolation followed by extraction, in an individual psychopath. The pattern transfers cleanly to a state's pressure campaign against a person abroad. The clinical claim never should, and this book borrows the shape only.

**Mao Zedong, "Problems of War and Strategy" (1938).** States in one line that the Party commands the gun and the gun must never command the Party, the whole logic of the commissar system compressed to a sentence. The line states a rule for a revolutionary party seizing power, not a rule any other kind of institution can adopt.

**Mao Zedong, "Introducing *The Communist*" (1939).** Names the united front as the first of the Party's three magic weapons, decades before the same instrument reached a diaspora abroad. It says nothing about what the instrument looks like once artificial intelligence runs its targeting.

**Frank Dikötter, *Mao's Great Famine* (2010).** Documents the deadliest famine in recorded history as the direct product of a coherent system with no channel for bad news to travel upward. The book stands as the clearest single proof that this book's master dimension has a body count when it runs unchecked.

**Frank Dikötter, *The Cultural Revolution* (2016).** Documents a civilization turned against its own teachers by the same instrument later aimed outward. It closes the case, argued throughout this book, that the Chinese people were this method's first subjects rather than its authors.

**Jonathan Phillips, *The Fourth Crusade and the Sack of Constantinople* (2004).** Traces the full Venice-Byzantium arc this book uses as its lifecycle anchor, from the 1082 trading privileges to the 1204 sack. It reads the collapse as one empire's failure of judgment, not as a five-stage pattern a modern institution might recognize in its own arrangements.

**Jack Weatherford, *Genghis Khan and the Making of the Modern World* (2004).** Recovers the *yam* relay network and the Mongols' use of merchants as intelligence, alongside the terror of the submit-or-be-destroyed ultimatum. It reads the empire as a story of conquest, not as an early demonstration of topology and psychological operations run as one instrument.

**National Institute of Standards and Technology, *Cybersecurity Framework 2.0* (2024).** Adds Governance as a sixth function beside the original five. Identify. Protect. Detect. Respond. Recover. The addition arrives at this book's own argument about coordination from a different direction entirely. The framework was built for a single organization's cyber posture, not for a civilization's whole contest.

**Department of Defense Architecture Framework, version 2.02.** Supplies the capability, operational, systems and data viewpoints this book borrows to show a military planner the framework in a vocabulary already his own. No one built it with a whole-of-society, peacetime, twelve-domain contest in view.

**MITRE Corporation, *ATT&CK* framework.** Proved that a shared taxonomy of adversary techniques turns one organization's observation into every other organization's warning, the model this book's own thirty-six stratagems follow. It catalogs techniques inside one domain, cyber, and this book is the argument for building the same catalog across twelve.

**Carnegie Mellon Software Engineering Institute, *Capability Maturity Model Integration*.** Supplies the five-level scale, Initial through Optimizing, this book borrows to grade a nation's cross-domain integration rather than a single organization's software process. No one ever built it to compare a free society against a totalitarian one, and this book's use of it is an adaptation, not an endorsement of the fit.

### A Note on This List

Twenty-five works cannot cover a subject this wide, and this appendix does not try. It gathers the sources this book leans on hardest, in one line each, so a reader who wants to go deeper on any single point knows exactly where to start. No entry ranks another. Several of these authors would disagree with each other, and a few would disagree with this book. The list is a shelf, not a verdict.
