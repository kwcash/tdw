## Appendix D. The Doctrine Crosswalk

*Mapping the Ten Dimensions Against Established Doctrine*

A military reader arrives at this book already owning a vocabulary. This appendix maps the ten Dimensions against three doctrine systems that reader already knows. The nine Principles of War, the Army's six warfighting functions, and joint doctrine's seven joint functions. Some Dimensions translate cleanly. Others do not, and the gap is itself a finding.

### The Three Systems, Briefly

**The nine Principles of War** are these. Objective. Offensive. Mass. Economy of Force. Maneuver. Unity of Command. Security. Surprise. Simplicity. \[1\] The list reached its current form in 1949 and has held since, the closest thing American doctrine has to a constitution for tactical and operational thought.

**The Army's six warfighting functions** are these. Command and Control. Movement and Maneuver. Intelligence. Fires. Sustainment. Protection. \[2\] The Army replaced an older set of eight Battlefield Operating Systems with this six-function structure in 2008. Treat the Battlefield Operating Systems as history. Nothing in this book's framework maps against them, and no current doctrine does either. One naming note matters for a reader working from an older manual. The first function was called Mission Command from 2008 until July 2019, when the Army restored the term Command and Control for the function itself, keeping mission command as the philosophy a commander applies through it. \[3\]

**Joint doctrine's seven joint functions** are these. Command and Control. Intelligence. Fires. Movement and Maneuver. Protection. Sustainment. Information. \[4\] Joint doctrine ran six functions, without Information, until 2017. A reader working from a source written before that date will find six, not seven, and should update the count rather than assume an error.

That 2017 addition is worth pausing on. The joint force concluded, independently of this book and years before it existed, that information had to stand as its own function rather than as a supporting task inside the other six. This book's own argument, that Agency and Resonance deserve standing as Dimensions in their own right rather than as footnotes to Fires or Intelligence, reached the same conclusion by a different road.

### The Crosswalk

| Dimension | Nearest Principle of War | Nearest Warfighting or Joint Function | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time | None directly | Sustainment | Loose |
| Entropy | Simplicity | Command and Control | Close |
| Topology | Unity of Command | Command and Control | Close |
| Asymmetry | Economy of Force, Maneuver | Movement and Maneuver | Close |
| Agency | Security | Information | Loose |
| Resonance | Unity of Command | Command and Control | Close |
| Phase | Surprise, Mass | Fires | Close |
| Gradient | None directly | Information | Loose |
| Criticality | Objective, Mass | Fires, Intelligence | Close |
| Coherence | Unity of Command | Command and Control | Closest |

Six of the ten sit close to doctrine already in place. Coherence maps almost exactly onto Unity of Command, restated at civilizational scale rather than at the scale of a single operation. Resonance is the same principle applied across domains instead of across units. Phase restates Surprise and Mass together, the timing half of each. Criticality restates Objective and Mass from the standpoint of the target rather than the attacker. Entropy and Topology both live inside what doctrine already calls Command and Control, read as friction and as shape respectively.

### Where the Fit Is Loose, and Why

Three Dimensions resist a clean mapping, and each resistance teaches something.

**Time** has no principle of war to answer to, because the principles assume two forces fighting on comparable horizons. A campaign has a beginning and an end, and the principles govern conduct inside that span. Time as this book uses it describes a mismatch between horizons themselves, a rival planning in decades against a rival planning in election cycles, and doctrine built for a bounded campaign has nothing to say about an unbounded one. Sustainment comes closest only because it is the function concerned with duration, not because it was built for this purpose.

**Agency** has no principle of war to answer to either, because the principles assume both sides know a contest is underway. A captured institution does not know it has been captured, and doctrine written to guide a commander who knows he is fighting has no native vocabulary for an actor who does not know it yet. Security comes closest, since Security is defined as denying the enemy an unexpected advantage, and institutional capture is exactly an advantage taken before the target notices the taking. The fit stays loose because Security, in its classical sense, still assumes an adversary relationship both parties recognize.

**Gradient** is the newest gap of the three. Doctrine built around force, the application or threat of it, has no principle for a slope built entirely from incentive. The target never has to be threatened at all, because compliance is the easier path on its own. The 2017 addition of Information as a joint function is the closest doctrine has come to naming this territory. Information is where an incentive-based influence operation would have to sit if joint doctrine tried to house it, but Information as currently defined is built around messaging and deception, not around the design of markets and institutions that Gradient describes.

None of these gaps is a defect in the Principles of War. The principles were never meant to answer a contest fought below the threshold of open war. Such a contest runs across financial, legal and cultural terrain in peacetime, waged by a party that treats every domain as one campaign. They are a defect only in the assumption that nine principles built for a bounded battle between uniformed forces would still cover a contest that has moved outside those bounds. The three loose fits mark exactly where the contest has moved.

### Reading a Case Chapter Against the Crosswalk

A military reader working through this book's case studies can use the table above as a translation key. Where a case chapter names Coherence as the signature gap, read Unity of Command, applied across departments rather than across units in the field. A case naming Phase calls for the Surprise and Mass a commander already knows, compressed from a battlefield timescale to a policy timescale. Agency or Gradient named in a case should signal no clean doctrinal analogue at all, since these two Dimensions are where this book's argument and existing doctrine most clearly diverge, and where a reader's own doctrinal instincts will least prepare him for what the case describes.

### Sourcing Status: Appendix D

**\[1\]** The nine Principles of War reached their current form in U.S. Army Field Service Regulations in 1949 and have been restated in every subsequent capstone doctrine publication since. The nine, in the order this book uses, are Objective. Offensive. Mass. Economy of Force. Maneuver. Unity of Command. Security. Surprise. Simplicity.

**\[2\]** Army Doctrine Publication 3-0 defines the six warfighting functions. The Army replaced the earlier eight Battlefield Operating Systems with this structure in 2008.

**\[3\]** *New.* Army Doctrine Publication 6-0 was published July 31, 2019. Its new title reads *Mission Command, Command and Control of Army Forces*. The revision restored Command and Control as the name of the warfighting function itself and retained mission command as the Army's philosophy for exercising it. The Army's own account of the change states plainly that the earlier single term had created ambiguity between the philosophy, the warfighting function and the supporting systems.

**\[4\]** *New.* Joint Publication 1 and Joint Publication 3-0 define the joint functions. Information was added as the seventh joint function in 2017, following a doctrine revision process that ran from 2016 into 2017. The six functions before that addition were these. Command and Control. Intelligence. Fires. Movement and Maneuver. Protection. Sustainment.

**Stated as inference, not finding.** Every entry in the crosswalk table is this book's own analytical judgment about which existing doctrinal category sits nearest a given Dimension. No cited military publication endorses this mapping, and a reader with deeper doctrinal training may reasonably draw some lines differently. The claim that Agency and Gradient mark genuine gaps in the Principles of War, rather than gaps in this book's own reading of them, is likewise an argument offered to the reader rather than a settled finding.
