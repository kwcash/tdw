## Appendix B. The Thirty-Six Stratagems in Full

*With the Complete Taiwan Mapping*

For two thousand years, Chinese statecraft has carried a codified set of thirty-six stratagems. \[1\] Western analysts often file them under philosophy or folklore. That filing is a category error. The stratagems are an engineered taxonomy of leverage, thirty-six tested methods for reaching a strategic objective through deception, indirect force and the exploitation of an opponent's own structure.

Through most of history, human friction capped their reach. Running one deception across the economic, informational and diplomatic domains at once took a bureaucracy that never tired and a network that never leaked. People tire. Networks leak. The stratagems worked, but the human mind set their speed limit. Artificial intelligence erases that limit. Fed into a system that processes a planet's data in real time, the thirty-six stop being history and become the operating software this book calls Total Domain War, running across all twelve domains at once.

Six of the thirty-six get a full profile below, one from each group, because they carry the most weight across this book's cases. The rest get a line apiece. Each entry carries its classical principle, a documented modern application and a Western wartime parallel. The point of this appendix is not that the stratagems are Chinese. These entries name the grammar of strategy, and the West has spoken that grammar fluently whenever it had to.

### The Architecture of Leverage: Six Groups

The thirty-six are not a flat list. They divide into six groups of six, and the division is not decorative. Each group fits a different position on the board. Reading which group an actor reaches for reads how that actor rates its own strength.

Groups I and II belong to the actor already holding the advantage. The work is Superiority and Confrontation, and the goal is to win cheaply, by misdirection rather than force. Groups III and IV belong to the contest itself. The work is Attack and Confusion, degrading an opponent's capability and fouling its decision loops before any decisive clash. Groups V and VI belong to the actor operating inside a stronger host, or under real pressure. The work is Control and Desperation, capturing a host's institutions from within, or retreating in good order to rebuild for the next round.

A Group I move says an actor believes it already holds the high ground. A Group VI move, including the instruction to retreat, says the actor is buying time. Naming the group names the phase of the campaign.

### Group I. Stratagems of Superiority

Deploy from strength. Win without unnecessary cost by weaponizing the advantage through misdirection rather than direct force.

**Stratagem 4. Wait at Ease for the Weary Enemy.** This is the group's anchor and the Time dimension made operational. The classical text holds the high ground, keeps fresh water and lets the enemy climb. The Party holds a centennial horizon, planning in decades and building in pipelines, while the American system plans in two-year electoral cycles and annual budgets. The Party does not need to defeat that system in the field. It waits while the system spends itself on cycles that reverse every few years. Admiral Nimitz ran the Western version of the same patience across the Pacific from 1943 onward, bypassing the strongest Japanese garrisons at Rabaul and Truk, cutting their supply and leaving tens of thousands of defenders to wait on islands the war had already passed by. \[2\]

The rest of the group. **1. Deceive the Heavens to Cross the Sea.** Hides the decisive act inside routine until repetition makes the observer stop looking. **2. Besiege Wei to Rescue Zhao.** Threatens what the enemy cannot afford to lose rather than striking its strength, the logic behind the Doolittle Raid of April 1942. \[3\] **3. Kill with a Borrowed Knife.** Lets a proxy deliver the strike and absorb the blame. **5. Loot a Burning House.** Advances while the adversary is consumed by its own crisis, the same logic behind the Destroyers-for-Bases deal of September 1940. \[4\] **6. Make a Sound in the East, Strike in the West.** Manufactures a loud threat in one place to strip the guard from another.

### Group II. Stratagems of Confrontation

Outmaneuver a stronger adversary through misdirection, manufactured reality and the exploitation of the adversary's own defensive logic.

**Stratagem 8. Openly Repair the Gallery Roads, March Secretly Through Chencang.** This is the anchor. Han Xin let the enemy watch his soldiers rebuild the mountain roads while his real force slipped through a pass hundreds of miles off. Two tracks run at once, one built to look real and one built to win. *Military-Civil Fusion* is the modern version, a visible commercial firm paired with a hidden legal duty that hands its technology to the state on demand. Montgomery built the Western equivalent at El Alamein in October 1942 with a dummy pipeline timed to suggest a southern attack while real armor massed disguised in the north. \[5\]

The rest of the group. **7. Create Something from Nothing.** Repeats a fiction at enough volume that it reads as fact, the same device the Ghost Army ran against German reconnaissance in 1944. \[6\] **9. Watch the Fire from Across the River.** Lets rivals destroy each other and advances into the ruin, the logic behind the Two-Ocean Navy Act of July 1940. \[7\] **10. Hide a Knife Behind a Smile.** Projects warmth until a structural trap closes. **11. Sacrifice the Plum Tree to Preserve the Peach.** Surrenders a lesser asset to shield the critical one. **12. Take the Opportunity to Pilfer a Goat.** Pockets every incidental gain until the aggregate decides the outcome, the same logic behind the Alsos Mission's trailing collection of scientists and equipment. \[9\]

### Group III. Stratagems of Attack

Use deception, sabotage and indirect force to degrade the adversary's capability before any decisive engagement.

**Stratagem 18. Capture the Ringleader to Disband the Bandits.** This is the anchor. The actor does not fight the marching mass. It finds the node that gives the network its coherence and takes that instead. Hong Kong in 2020 ran this to the letter, jailing a publisher, freezing a newspaper's payroll and detaining the organizers rather than arresting a million marchers. American codebreakers ran the identical logic against Admiral Yamamoto in April 1943, intercepting his aircraft rather than attempting to degrade the fleet he commanded. \[10\]

The rest of the group. **13. Stomp the Grass to Scare the Snake.** Pressures a visible node so a hidden network moves and reveals its shape. **14. Borrow a Corpse to Resurrect the Soul.** Inhabits a hollow institution and runs new objectives on its residual legitimacy, the device behind Operation Mincemeat's fictional officer in 1943. \[11\] **15. Lure the Tiger Down the Mountain.** Draws the adversary onto terrain the actor controls. **16. Let the Enemy Off in Order to Catch Them.** Eases pressure to induce overextension, then tightens. Eisenhower ran this logic against the German counteroffensive in the Ardennes in December 1944. **17. Cast a Brick to Attract Jade.** Offers something of modest value to extract something far greater, the same trade Britain's Tizard Mission made in 1940. \[12\]

### Group IV. Stratagems of Confusion

Destabilize the adversary's operating environment through manufactured chaos and the removal of the foundations its power depends on.

**Stratagem 24. Borrow the Road to Conquer Guo.** This is the anchor. The classical actor asks passage through a neighbor's land under a neutral purpose, then uses the road for a second purpose the neighbor never granted. Telecommunications equipment sold as ordinary commerce carries an embedded duty, owed to the state under fusion law, to enable access the buyer never agreed to host. BGI's prenatal testing business, developed with the People's Liberation Army and marketed under a purely medical banner, runs the identical logic through a different door. \[8\] Britain and the Soviet Union ran the same logic against Iran in August 1941, opening a supply corridor that became an occupation for the war's duration and after. \[13\]

The rest of the group. **19. Remove the Firewood from Under the Pot.** Cuts the fuel that sustains a capability rather than attacking the capability directly. **20. Muddy the Waters to Catch the Fish.** Floods the field with noise until threat cannot be told from clutter, the same effect RAF chaff had over Hamburg in July 1943. \[14\] **21. Slough Off the Cicada's Golden Shell.** Leaves a convincing decoy in place while the real actor relocates undetected. **22. Shut the Door to Catch the Thief.** Encircles completely before engaging, the logic behind closing the Falaise Gap in August 1944. **23. Befriend a Distant State While Attacking a Neighbor.** Buys distant alignment to strip the target of a coalition it would otherwise call on.

### Group V. Stratagems of Control

From growing strength, infiltrate, replace and redirect the adversary's power structures until they serve the actor's own objectives.

**Stratagem 30. Make the Host and the Guest Exchange Places.** This is the anchor and the Agency dimension in practice. The move is one a target least expects, precisely because it is slow. The guest arrives deferential and dependent, and over years gathers relationships and institutional knowledge until it becomes indispensable, at which point it sets the agenda. Two decades inside the international standards bodies is the macro version this book's *Protocols and Ports* documents in full, delegates entering as observers and departing as drafters. \[15\] The United States played the identical role inside the Anglo-American alliance. It arrived in 1942 as the newer, deferential partner. By 1944 it was setting Overlord's terms over British planners' own preferences.

The rest of the group. **25. Replace the Beams with Rotten Timbers.** Swaps the load-bearing supports of an institution for compromised ones that hold until real weight applies. **26. Point at the Mulberry While Cursing the Locust.** Punishes a secondary target so the real audience reads the warning unaddressed. **27. Feign Madness Without Losing Balance.** Projects weakness so the adversary underestimates real capability. **28. Lure the Enemy onto the Roof, Then Remove the Ladder.** Induces dependency, then cuts the way back, the same sequence that deepened Japan's oil dependence before the 1941 cutoff. **29. Deck the Tree with False Blossoms.** Projects more strength or coalition than the actor holds, the device behind the fictional Fortitude divisions of 1944.

### Group VI. Stratagems of Desperate Situations

From disadvantage, use layered deception and the adversary's own assumptions to survive and reconstitute for the next engagement.

**Stratagem 36. If All Else Fails, Retreat.** This is the anchor. Alone among the thirty-six it names withdrawal as doctrine rather than failure. A disciplined retreat preserves the force for the next cycle. When the Party pulls back from a market or abandons a deal, the correct reading is repositioning, not defeat. American forces learned the same doctrine at Kasserine Pass in February 1943. \[16\] Command did not score the retreat as the campaign's end. It reorganized and returned weeks later to defeat the same army on the same ground.

The rest of the group. **31. The Beauty Trap.** Compromises a decision-maker through whatever desire makes that person susceptible. **32. The Empty Fortress.** Projects such confidence from real weakness that the adversary assumes a trap and declines to press, the posture Britain held after Dunkirk. **33. Sow Discord Among the Enemy.** Finds the existing fractures in an adversary's coalition and widens them. **34. Inflict Injury on Yourself to Win Trust.** Accepts visible damage to establish credibility and gain access otherwise unavailable, the method behind the Double-Cross System's controlled losses. \[17\] **35. Chain the Stratagems.** Links every other entry into one sequence, and gets its own section next.

### The Meta-Stratagem: Chaining

Stratagem 35 is the most important of the thirty-six, because no single stratagem alone produces the campaigns this book documents. Each case chapter names a chain, a sequence of these techniques run across domains where one entry's success is the condition the next one needs. The Allies chained the same way to open the decisive front in Europe. Stratagem 1 hid the invasion's true preparation inside routine movement orders. Stratagem 29 decked the northern flank with a fictional army threatening Norway. Stratagem 14 had already misdirected German reinforcement toward Sardinia and Greece over a year earlier. Stratagem 22 closed the door at Falaise only once the landing the earlier links had protected had succeeded. No single deception won Normandy. The chain did, exactly as this stratagem describes.

### Sourcing Status: Appendix B

**\[1\]** *Thirty-Six Stratagems* (三十六計), traditional. The received text dates to the Ming or late Qing period, with individual stratagems drawn from far older sources including the *Book of Qi* and the *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*.

**\[2\]** The bypassing of the Japanese garrisons at Rabaul and Truk, 1943 to 1944, is documented across standard U.S. Navy operational histories of the Pacific campaign.

**\[3\]** The Doolittle Raid of April 18, 1942 and the Battle of Midway of June 4 to 7, 1942 are both extensively documented in the standard military histories of the Pacific War.

**\[4\]** The Destroyers-for-Bases Agreement was concluded on September 2, 1940.

**\[5\]** Operation Bertram, the deception plan supporting the Second Battle of El Alamein, ran in October 1942.

**\[6\]** The 23rd Headquarters Special Troops, the unit known as the Ghost Army, operated from 1944 to 1945. Its records were declassified in 1996.

**\[7\]** The Two-Ocean Navy Act is Public Law 76-757. Congress passed it on July 19, 1940.

**\[8\]** BGI Group's NIFTY prenatal test and its documented collaboration with the People's Liberation Army are covered in published research co-authorship and in public statements from the U.S. National Counterintelligence and Security Center. The precise count of women tested and the program's exact start date vary across sources, so this appendix states the scale qualitatively rather than with a specific figure.

**\[9\]** The Alsos Mission operated from 1943 to 1945, documented in National Archives Record Group 165.

**\[10\]** Operation Vengeance intercepted Admiral Yamamoto's aircraft on April 18, 1943.

**\[11\]** Operation Mincemeat ran from April to July 1943.

**\[12\]** The Tizard Mission traveled to the United States in September 1940.

**\[13\]** Operation Countenance was the Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran. It ran from August 25 to September 17, 1941.

**\[14\]** Operation Gomorrah was the July 1943 bombing campaign over Hamburg. It saw the first operational use of Window, the codename for radar-jamming chaff.

**\[15\]** The full documentation of two decades of positional movement inside the ITU, ISO and 3GPP standards bodies appears in *Protocols and Ports*.

**\[16\]** The Battle of Kasserine Pass ran from February 19 to 24, 1943. Patton assumed command of II Corps on March 6, 1943.

**\[17\]** The Double-Cross System's official history has been released in full through the National Archives.

**Naming decisions applied throughout this book.** *Stratagem 9* is rendered Watch the Fire from Across the River. This is the closer rendering of 隔岸觀火, to observe a fire from the opposite bank, and it is standardized against an earlier variant. *Stratagem 14* is rendered Borrow a Corpse to Resurrect the Soul, standardized against an earlier variant that read Raise the Spirit. *Stratagem 30* is rendered Make the Host and the Guest Exchange Places. An earlier variant read turn the host into the guest, which inverts the actor, since in 反客為主 it is the guest who rises. All three corrections apply everywhere the stratagem appears in this book.

**Group structure, for reference.** Group I holds Stratagems 1 through 6, anchored on 4. Group II holds 7 through 12, anchored on 8. Group III holds 13 through 18, anchored on 18. Group IV holds 19 through 24, anchored on 24. Group V holds 25 through 30, anchored on 30. Group VI holds 31 through 36, anchored on 36.

**Stated as inference, not finding.** The claim that Huawei and ZTE base stations carry access capability owed to the state under fusion law is an argument from the 2017 National Intelligence Law's documented legal obligation, not from a demonstrated technical finding in any specific deployed equipment. What a particular base station does in practice is not publicly documented, and this appendix does not assert otherwise.

**On the Western parallels.** The historical episodes cited alongside each stratagem are standard, widely documented military history. They are drawn from operational histories and official records rather than from specialized or contested scholarship. None were independently re-verified against primary sources for this edition beyond the citations above, consistent with their status as settled history rather than live reporting.
