## Appendix A. The Stack in Full

*The Reference Architecture*

Enterprise architecture is the discipline that makes a large organization act as one. It lines up the strategy, the functions, the data and the technology so every part pulls toward a single aim.

That makes it the engineering of coherence, and Coherence is the dimension this book keeps returning to, the one the Party holds and the West lacks. Set the framework down as an architecture and it becomes something a person can build rather than only argue for. The Stack in the front matter introduced this briefly. This appendix gives it in full.

Total Domain War is an enterprise, not by metaphor but by structure. It pursues one aim across many functions, on a shared base of technology and data. The Party runs it as a single coherent system. The West runs the same functions as a scatter of projects owned by rival institutions that answer to none of the others.

The gap between them is not a gap in money or talent. Architecture gaps close with architecture, and this appendix specifies the one this book proposes.

### The Unifying Stack

The three catalogs at the center of this book are not three subjects. They are three views of one architecture, mapped onto the layers an architect already works in.

The ten **Dimensions** are the Architecture Principles, the physics every design must obey. The twelve **Domains** are the Business Architecture, the functions the enterprise performs. The thirty-six **Stratagems** are the Application Architecture, the patterns those functions run on. Full detail on the Stratagems, including the complete Taiwan mapping, lives in Appendix B. Full detail on the Dimensions and Domains lives in Appendix C.

Beneath these sit the technology layers this book calls the Digital Twin and the orchestration layer, treated at length in that case chapter. The Digital Twin is the Data Architecture, the working model of a target assembled from the harvest. Agentic AI is the orchestration layer, running the technique catalog across domains faster than a human staff can answer. Compute, network and cyber infrastructure sit beneath both.

Above every layer sits governance, which is Coherence doing the architect's work, holding every layer to one aim. *Coherence Without Command* argues that governance at the scale this contest requires must run on architecture contracts and an architecture board rather than on command, and this appendix does not repeat that argument. It only notes where governance sits in the stack.

Read the stack top to bottom and it forms a sentence. Principles govern functions. Functions run on techniques. Techniques run on data through orchestration. Governance holds the whole in line.

The Party's enterprise reads straight down that sentence. The West's breaks at the last line. It holds the functions, most of the technology and the finest individual talent in the world. It fields no architect with authority over the whole, so its layers do not line up. The failure the architecture reveals is not a missing capability. A missing governance layer, precisely specified, is the actual defect.

### The Ontology

A separate enterprise-architecture framework, the Zachman Framework, tests whether a model is complete by asking six questions of it. What. How. Where. Who. When. Why. \[1\] This book's framework answers each one without strain, which is evidence the model is complete rather than an arbitrary list.

**What** is the data, the Digital Twin. **How** is the Stratagem catalog. **Where** is Topology, the shape power flows through. **Who** is Agency and Association, the people and the organizations. **When** is Time and Phase, the horizon and the beat. **Why** is Coherence, binding every part to one aim.

Two of those six answers are whole Dimensions rather than borrowed concepts, Topology and Time. An architecture built for a contest fought across geography and across decades puts the where and the when in the foundation rather than in the trim.

### The Capability Maturity Model

Engineering grades not only what an organization does but how maturely it does it. The Capability Maturity Model Integration, a standard developed at Carnegie Mellon, runs five levels. Initial. Managed. Defined. Quantitatively Managed. Optimizing. \[2\] Laid against Total Domain War, the same five levels grade an actor's ability to wage or withstand the contest. The abstract Coherence gap becomes something an engineer can track rather than only argue.

The grade is uncomfortable to state plainly. In single technical domains, cyber and finance and advanced computing alike, the West runs at or near the top of the scale. Per-domain skill was never the problem this book documents.

In cross-domain integration, the capability that decides outcomes, the West runs near the bottom. It fields no architect to define the higher-order processes. Assessments get produced but are not comparable across the agencies that produce them. Responses get executed but are not sequenced against each other. Nothing is repeatable across agencies, because nothing is specified across agencies. The Party runs near the top of the integration scale, not because its domains are individually stronger but because they answer to one enterprise.

The maturity gap is the Coherence gap, made legible on a scale an engineer already knows how to read. That low integration cancels a capability lead rather than merely discounting it is this book's model, argued throughout its case studies, not a measurement this appendix reports as settled.

### The Technique Matrix, in Brief

Cyber defense changed in practice once it stopped logging incidents one at a time and started mapping adversary behavior to a shared matrix of tactics and techniques, the MITRE ATT&CK framework. \[3\] A common vocabulary let one organization's observation become another's warning.

The thirty-six Stratagems are that kind of artifact, roughly two thousand years older. They sort into six groups keyed to the actor's position. Advantage when ahead. Confrontation in a balanced fight. Offense against a weaker foe. Exploiting chaos among several parties. Combination to gain ground. Retreat when behind. Appendix B lays out all thirty-six in that order, with the full mapping against the Taiwan case.

Set out as a matrix rather than read as a classical curiosity, the thirty-six become an operational map instead. Every case in this book's third and fourth parts is a path through that matrix, a sequence of techniques across domains, the same way an intrusion is a path through the cyber matrix. A reader who knows the matrix can name the technique in play in a live case and anticipate the next one, because the library is finite and the six groups run in a known sequence.

### The Military Viewpoints

Defense staffs already own architecture frameworks built to model an enterprise of exactly this kind, through a set of standard viewpoints, including the Department of Defense Architecture Framework. \[4\] This book's framework fills them without strain.

The **capability view** takes the Dimensions and the maturity grade above. The **operational view** takes the twelve Domains. The **systems view** takes the AI, data, network and cyber layers. The **data view** takes the Digital Twin.

This is the plain answer to whether military planners could use this framework starting tomorrow. They could, because the tools, the viewpoints and the staffs trained on them are already theirs. The obstacle this book documents throughout is not capability. Authority is the obstacle, the subject *Coherence Without Command* takes up directly.

### The Defensive Architecture

The architecture organizes the defense as well as the threat it describes. NIST's Cybersecurity Framework, updated to version 2.0 in February 2024, now runs six functions instead of the five its first version specified. The sixth is Governance, added because practitioners found the same gap this book finds. \[5\] The counters scattered through this book's case studies assemble into that same six-function shape, and each is discharged by a dimensional move already named in this appendix.

**Identify.** Know what you hold and where it is exposed. This is Criticality applied inward, the chokepoint audit that finds the rare-earth dependency, the single-source input and the utility whose failure cascades. The counters in the Economic and Financial cases are largely Identify functions.

**Protect.** Reduce the exposure the audit finds. Second sources and stockpiles, ownership requirements and research-security standards, design rules that protect minors. This is Gradient run in reverse, flattening a slope the adversary would otherwise ride down.

**Detect.** See the operation while it runs. This is a Phase and Topology problem, and it is where the West is weakest. Detection across domains requires that a securities filing, a patent volume, a recruitment offer and a network intrusion be describable in one schema. At present they are not.

**Respond.** Act inside the adversary's decision cycle. This is Phase again, and it requires the pre-authorization argued for across several cases, because a response designed during a crisis arrives after the crisis has already been decided.

**Recover.** Restore function and, harder, restore trust. This is Coherence, and the domestic cases show why recovery is the least tractable of the six. A department can remove thousands of people by order and cannot bring their trust back by the same order, because trust is not a switch any authority holds.

**Govern.** Set the strategy, the roles and the risk tolerance the other five run inside. NIST added this function because an organization can run the other five well at the working level and still fail without one layer above all five holding them to a single aim. This is Coherence again, named for a second time because the same gap shows up at every scale this book examines, from a single firm's cybersecurity program to a free society's whole contest with the Party.

Assembled, the six are a defensive architecture in their own right, and they show the same truth from the protective side that the offense showed from the other. Every function but the narrowly technical depends on a coordinating authority the West does not currently field.

### The Principles Table

TOGAF documents architecture principles in a fixed form. A name, a statement, a rationale and the implications that follow. The ten Dimensions have been documented informally throughout this book. They receive that formal treatment here for the first time, in one place.

#### Time

**Statement.** The actor holding the longer strategic horizon accumulates a decisive advantage against an actor constrained to shorter political or fiscal cycles.

**Rationale.** *We Built This* documents an Enclosure accumulated over roughly a century, one defensible decision at a time, against a Party outlook stated in terms of a 2049 aim. A century-long process and a four-year electoral cycle are not different speeds on the same clock. They are different clocks.

**Implications.** Institutions that reset their priorities with each electoral or budget cycle cede this Dimension by default, regardless of any single cycle's resourcing. A defense that only ever plans to the next cycle has already lost the Dimension before the contest inside that cycle begins.

#### Entropy

**Statement.** An actor that exports disorder while preserving order at home gains at the rate the disorder it exports spreads.

**Rationale.** *Accommodate* documents a domestic feed governed by a registry and a regulator, set against covert networks abroad that amplify division on several sides of a question at once. The order and the disorder are the same design, aimed in two directions.

**Implications.** Entropy must be measured bidirectionally. The question is never only whether disorder exists in a society, since some disorder is normal in any free one. The question is who controls its direction and its rate, and whether the same actor that benefits from disorder abroad tolerates none of it at home.

#### Topology

**Statement.** A centralized structure concentrates the coordination that a distributed structure disperses, and the two cannot be judged against the same standard of success.

**Rationale.** The Party's enterprise runs as a hub, one center connecting every agency and enterprise this book documents. The West's institutions run as a mesh, independent nodes answering to no shared center. *Coherence Without Command* argues that the mesh's resilience is inseparable from its coordination cost.

**Implications.** A fix imported from a hub-shaped system, a single command authority, will not survive contact with a mesh. It should not be attempted. The correct fix works with the mesh's own Topology, an architecture contract that coordinates without commanding, rather than against it.

#### Asymmetry

**Statement.** An actor unable to win a contest at one altitude will move the contest to an altitude where the balance favors it instead.

**Rationale.** *Total Domain War, Not Asymmetric War* documents the literature on how weaker actors defeat stronger ones. American supremacy is total in the kinetic domain. This book's cases show the same actor moving the contest instead into financial, legal and informational domains, where that supremacy does not reach as cleanly.

**Implications.** Overwhelming strength in one domain does not deter action in another, and a defender who aggregates strength across all domains into one confidence score will miss exactly the domain an adversary has chosen to fight in. Assessment has to run domain by domain.

#### Agency

**Statement.** The contest's actual target is a population's or an individual's capacity to decide, not that population's belief in any single fact.

**Rationale.** *Accommodate* documents target audience analysis aimed at shaping a decision rather than correcting or replacing a specific belief. A population that believes every fact correctly but cannot act coherently on those facts has still lost the Dimension.

**Implications.** A defense built only to correct false claims, a fact-check or a correction notice, treats the symptom and misses the target. Resilience has to address the capacity to decide, which is a different capacity than the capacity to know.

#### Resonance

**Statement.** An intervention that amplifies an existing, organic division compounds faster and lasts longer than one that manufactures a division from nothing.

**Rationale.** *Accommodate* argues that the platform and the practitioner both find division rather than create it, and that the *COVID-19* years deepened a distrust the Party did not originate.

**Implications.** A society's own baseline of cohesion is a defense-relevant variable in its own right, not a separate question from national security. The seed this Dimension describes dies in soil that was never prepared for it.

#### Phase

**Statement.** The actor that completes its decision cycle faster inside a fixed window captures the advantage regardless of which actor holds the greater resources.

**Rationale.** *Protocols and Ports* documents a Party veto inserted twenty-two days after an American order, against a divestiture that took five and a half years. *Military-Civil Fusion* and *Taiwan* both show the same gap between an adversary's weeks and a defender's years.

**Implications.** An approval chain built to prevent error becomes, unmodified, a vulnerability once an adversary's cycle time is measured in weeks. Pre-authorization, deciding the response before the crisis rather than during it, is the only countermeasure this book documents that does not simply abandon deliberation.

#### Gradient

**Statement.** Power flows toward the actor holding the steepest incentive slope, and the flow requires no coercion once the slope exists.

**Rationale.** *Financial Control* and *Attract* both document capture that required no persuasion of any individual. An ordinary professional incentive did the whole job, already pointed in one direction by a fee, a bonus or an index inclusion.

**Implications.** A defense built only against explicit coercion, a threat or a bribe, misses the majority of the cases this book documents. These cases ran entirely on incentive rather than on any threat. The fix has to change the slope itself, through disclosure or a screen at the point of decision, not merely police the threats that never had to be made.

#### Criticality

**Statement.** A chokepoint's value is set by what depends on it, not by its own size, cost or visibility.

**Rationale.** *Protocols and Ports* and *Taiwan* both document small, unglamorous chokepoints. A crane, a coding standard, a fabrication process each carry leverage entirely disproportionate to their footprint.

**Implications.** An inventory of a society's own dependencies matters at least as much as an inventory of an adversary's capabilities, and ordinarily gets far less attention, because a dependency is boring in a way a capability is not.

#### Coherence

**Statement.** A system that aligns every domain under one aim multiplies its component advantages. A system that runs each domain separately cancels those same advantages before an adversary has to intervene.

**Rationale.** This is the master Dimension, and *Coherence Without Command* is this book's fullest argument for it. Every case in this book documents a domain where the West holds individual strength that its own lack of coordination cancels before the contest is even joined.

**Implications.** Coordination itself, not any single domain's resourcing, is the primary lever available to the West. The fix is architectural, an architecture contract and an architecture board. Nothing additive works instead, since no quantity of additional per-domain resourcing substitutes for the governance layer this book finds missing throughout.

### Where Governance Sits

Every architecture needs an architect, a function with authority across the whole enterprise to set the principles, hold the standards and keep the layers aligned. Without that authority, an architecture is a document rather than a system.

This book's own Topology predicts the difficulty precisely. The West is, by its structure, a mesh of independent nodes. Separated powers. Rival services. Autonomous agencies. Sovereign allies. None holds authority over the rest. The structure that keeps a free society free is the same structure that denies it a commanding architect, and *Coherence Without Command* argues at length that this is not a flaw to be engineered away but the design constraint any workable answer has to respect.

This appendix does not repeat that argument. It records only where governance sits in the stack. Above every layer, exercised through an architecture board with authority to convene and to publish, and no authority to command.

**\[1\]** John Zachman published the framework bearing his name in an IBM Systems Journal article in 1987, organized around six interrogatives applied across multiple perspectives.

**\[2\]** The Capability Maturity Model Integration is maintained by the CMMI Institute, a subsidiary of ISACA, building on a model originally developed at Carnegie Mellon University's Software Engineering Institute.

**\[3\]** The MITRE ATT&CK framework is maintained by the MITRE Corporation and is in wide use across the cybersecurity industry as a shared taxonomy of adversary tactics and techniques.

**\[4\]** The Department of Defense Architecture Framework models a defense enterprise through capability and operational viewpoints, systems and data viewpoints, and several others.

**\[5\]** *New.* The National Institute of Standards and Technology published Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 on February 26, 2024. It added Govern as a sixth core function. The other five stayed as they were. Identify. Protect. Detect. Respond. Recover. The first edition of this book, and the source material behind this appendix, predates that update and cited only five functions. This appendix corrects the count and treats the addition of Govern as independent confirmation of this book's own argument, made without reference to it.

**Corrections from the first edition.** The maturity-model level names are corrected to CMMI's current terminology. Initial. Managed. Defined. Quantitatively Managed. Optimizing. These replace the first edition's use of the older Capability Maturity Model's terms. The defensive-architecture section is expanded from five functions to six to match NIST's 2024 update. The count of intelligence organizations, where it recurs in this appendix's source material, follows the correction made throughout this book. Eighteen replaces seventeen.

**Stated as inference, not finding.** The claim that low cross-domain integration cancels a capability lead, rather than merely discounting it, is this book's model. This book's case studies examine that model throughout. No cited source reports it as a measurement.
